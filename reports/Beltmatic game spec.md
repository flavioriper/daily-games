# Beltmatic rebuilt: nine buildings, one hub, 32-bit arithmetic

Beltmatic (Notional Games, Steam app 2674590, released 29 March 2024, US$6.99, Windows only, Unity) is a factory game whose only resource is the integer itself: **nine placeable things** (belt, bridge, extractor, adder, multiplier, subtractor, divider, exponentiator, storage) feed a fixed **6×6 hub with 24 input faces**, every item is a signed 32-bit integer that **saturates at 2,147,483,647**, and progress is paid entirely by delivering specific numbers in specific counts, with no currency and no placement cost. Belts move **1 tile/s at level 1**, +1/s a level to 8/s at level 8, then +0.1/s to **20/s at level 128**; the adder runs 0.25 ops/s at level 1 and 4/s at level 108; the four other operators run 0.2/s to 3/s. The hub ladder hands out every building by level 22 (adder 1, bridge 4, multiplier 7, priorities 10, subtractor 13, divider 16, exponentiator 19, storage 22) and thereafter only a new extractable value a level, under the rule Extraction = Level − 7 from level 23; the hub's level cap is 1000 and targets are random per save, growing about 8.7% a level in the seventies but never exceeding 10^8 through level 1000. The sources are a community wiki last edited October 2024, one measured blog, two Steam guides (both now carrying a "removed from community" notice), developer forum replies, the Steam APIs and sampled store screenshots; ten patches since launch (latest 1.0.10, 18 April 2026) changed only input ergonomics, so every figure here describes the live rules. Where the notes disagree (belt base speed, the 1.0.10 year, the machine input buffer, the hub's belt count, the divide-by-zero remainder), this report states both readings and picks the one the evidence supports; what no source records (exponent edge cases, map generation, the target generator past level 40, per-rank costs past level 9) is marked as not found.

## Nine placeable buildings and one hub make the whole toolset

The inventory is small and closed. The Steam guide lists the unlock order as Belt and Extractor from the start, then Adder, Belt Bridge, Belt Priorities, Subtractor, Divider, Exponentiator and Storage ([Supposer, How to Play](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3391840619)); the wiki's Operators page puts the five arithmetic machines at **Adder L1, Multiplier L7, Subtractor L13, Divider L16, Exponentiator L19**, and classes Hub, Belt, Extractor, Bridge and Storage as machines that "act on numbers but do not change their value" ([Wiki Operators](https://beltmatic.fandom.com/wiki/Operators)). There is **no splitter, merger, balancer, underground belt, trash or void**: "Due to the fact that there are no built-in splitters within Beltmatic, to get an even output that is space efficient, the optimal route is to split the belt into 3 paths. The game will automatically split evenly between them" ([Wiki Adder](https://beltmatic.fandom.com/wiki/Adder)). Splitting and merging are belt junctions, buffering and balancing are Storage, and sinking is the Hub. Every operator takes exactly two inputs, A and B, and only the Divider has two outputs ([vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/2674590/beltmatic.md)). No source in any of the four notes names a placement cost, a build limit or a demolish refund; the nearest statement is that "Upgrades cost no currency, only large quantities of up to three different numbers" ([plannedallalong](http://plannedallalong.blogspot.com/2025/08/quick-review-beltmatic.html)) and a reviewer's "Because nothing has a cost" ([Steam reviews API](https://store.steampowered.com/appreviews/2674590?json=1&language=english&num_per_page=100&filter=recent)). Treat building as free and unlimited; it is inferred from silence, not confirmed.

Footprints and tile maps come from the wiki infobox images, read on 2026-10-15, corroborated by the blog's shape descriptions ([biggieblog](https://biggieblog.com/building-number-factories-in-beltmatic/)). Input tiles are the darker squares lettered A or B, output tiles the lighter ones printed with the formula ("Dark squares marked with a letter are inputs. Lighter squares are outputs", [Supposer](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3391840619)). Each input tile accepts a belt on any of its free faces and each output tile emits on any free face, so one machine can be fed from several sides and its result taken from whichever side is convenient.

The face counts are a geometric check on the sources: a 3×3 square exposes 4 × 3 = 12 faces and a 6×6 square 24, which is why the Extractor and Storage pages say 12 sides and the Hub page says 24 ([Wiki Extractor](https://beltmatic.fandom.com/wiki/Extractor); [Wiki Storage](https://beltmatic.fandom.com/wiki/Storage); [Wiki Hub](https://beltmatic.fandom.com/wiki/Hub)). The Divider page says A and B have "three faces" each, a sentence copied from the 1×3 Multiplier page; in the 2×2 image every tile has two exposed faces, so the image wins ([Wiki Divider](https://beltmatic.fandom.com/wiki/Divider)). The Bridge's infobox says unlock level 0 but the Levels table and the Steam guide both say level 4 ([Wiki Levels](https://beltmatic.fandom.com/wiki/Levels)); it "matches whatever speed the belts are running at and has no direct upgrades" ([Wiki Bridge](https://beltmatic.fandom.com/wiki/Bridge)). Machines rotate clockwise with R and counter-clockwise with Shift+R, and whole pasted selections rotate with R ([Supposer](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3391840619)); mirroring does not exist and is the single most-requested editing feature ([Steam reviews, top rated](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2674590/reviews?browsefilter=toprated)).

The Extractor is the one building whose rate is not its own. It "has 12 sides, all of which will output the number being extracted. If the number of sides being used exceeds the max belts/s upgrade level, the extracted numbers will be distributed evenly across the total number of belts being used"; its level is a count of belts (**1 belt at L1, +1 a level, cap 12**), and "the extractor doesn't extract 'X numbers/s', instead it is counted on fully saturated belts, whether those belts are running at 1/s or 10/s" ([Wiki Extractor](https://beltmatic.fandom.com/wiki/Extractor)). An extractor "stops feeding a belt that is backed up and sends more to other belts", and one at 8 belts on 8/s belts yielded 64 values/s ([biggieblog](https://biggieblog.com/building-number-factories-in-beltmatic/)). So an extractor's output is **min(connected belts, level) × belt speed**, spread evenly over every connected belt and redistributed away from any that is full; upgrading belts upgrades every extractor for free. Storage "allows you to store up to 10,000 of a single number. All 12 sides of the Storage can be either input or output, which is solely decided based on the direction of the belt that is connected to that side. There is no cap to the input or output speed ... All output belts coming from the Storage exit fully saturated, meaning it can act as a good buffer and belt balancing mechanism" ([Wiki Storage](https://beltmatic.fandom.com/wiki/Storage)); the stored value is "set by the first number it receives while empty" ([Supposer](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3391840619)), and the C hotkey clears numbers from an area including storage ([biggieblog](https://biggieblog.com/building-number-factories-in-beltmatic/)). The Hub "takes up a 6x6 (36) tile area and contains a total of 24 sides, all of which are inputs"; it shows the current target, delivered/required, the level and the next unlock, and numbers "can be created using extractors, stored using storage, and deleted by the hub" ([Wiki Hub](https://beltmatic.fandom.com/wiki/Hub); [Wiki Number](https://beltmatic.fandom.com/wiki/Number)).

## Integer arithmetic saturates at 2,147,483,647 and never emits a zero remainder

Each operation pops one number from A and one from B and emits one result (two for the Divider). Addition and multiplication are commutative and the wiki says so for both ([Wiki Adder](https://beltmatic.fandom.com/wiki/Adder); [Wiki Multiplier](https://beltmatic.fandom.com/wiki/Multiplier)). Subtraction is **A − B**, "the order of the inputs matters and negative numbers can result, although negative numbers are never required. They act just as normal negative numbers would in any mathematical operation" ([Wiki Subtractor](https://beltmatic.fandom.com/wiki/Subtractor)). Division emits **⌊A ÷ B⌋ on the A÷B tile and the remainder on the R tile**, "A÷B rounded down, plus remainder R (both outputs must be taken)" ([Supposer](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3391840619)), with the worked example "5÷2=2, with remainder of 1" ([plannedallalong](http://plannedallalong.blogspot.com/2025/08/quick-review-beltmatic.html)). Exponentiation is **A^B with A the base**, A being the tile at the top of the L and B the one at the left; "the order of the inputs matters greatly" ([Wiki Exponentiator](https://beltmatic.fandom.com/wiki/Exponentiator)). Because A and B are fixed tiles of the machine's shape, operand order is a placement and rotation decision, never a timing one.

Two Divider rules matter for a rebuild. First, **a zero remainder is not emitted**: "If the division has no remainder, it won't bother to make a 0 (even with space for it)" ([biggieblog](https://biggieblog.com/building-number-factories-in-beltmatic/)), corroborated by a player halving 50706: "you'd get the expected 25353 r0 ... this division will not output the remainder of 0" ([Yugo2Slow, "Zero!"](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2674590/discussions/0/4427688336540633246/)). A divisible pair therefore yields one item, not two, and an unconnected R output never stalls a divider that only ever divides evenly. Second, **division by zero does not fault**: "Division by 0 is apparently fine and dandy, and will output 0 as the quotient and the dividing number as the remainder" ([biggieblog](https://biggieblog.com/building-number-factories-in-beltmatic/)). "The dividing number" is ambiguous, and the strategy note read it as the divisor; the machines note read it against two in-game observations and found the dividend. Yugo2Slow's "If you divide 0 by 50706 you will get 0 r 50706, very useable" involves the operands {50706, 0} and the outputs (0, 50706); under ordinary modulus 0 ÷ 50706 would give R = 0, which the game never emits, so the player is describing 50706 ÷ 0 → quotient 0, remainder 50706. Lord Balder built a sequencer on the same quirk: "I get 2 outputs: one that is counted and eventually blocked, a second that continues to trigger divisions, whose value conveniently is also zero" ([Compact MAM Designs](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2674590/discussions/0/4548155824440414415/)), which is 0 ÷ 0 → (0, 0), again R = A. **The rule the evidence favours is A ÷ 0 → quotient 0, remainder A, both emitted even when A = 0.** No developer statement exists; if Yugo2Slow's sentence is read literally it would mean R = B whenever A = 0, which nothing else supports.

Values are 32-bit signed integers. "Their maximum value is 2,147,483,647 and their minimum is -2,147,483,648, since they are internally stored with a 32-bit integer limit" ([Wiki Number](https://beltmatic.fandom.com/wiki/Number)); "Any sufficiently large output is replaced by 2147483647, which is 2^31 – 1" and "In the negative direction, you get -2147483648" ([biggieblog](https://biggieblog.com/building-number-factories-in-beltmatic/)); "Eventually the goal hits 2147483647, the limit of numbers in the game" ([thebigh, 2024-08-06](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2674590/discussions/0/4515506995733927660/)). The top end **saturates rather than wraps** ("replaced by"). Two sources say 2^32 instead: vaporlens' "natural integers with an upper bound of 2^32 − 1" and a May 2024 player who built every power of 2 "up to 2^32" and claimed "any number from 1 to 4294967293" ([how to "make" the numbers?](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2674590/discussions/0/4364628453800871481/)); the blog is the only direct observation of the clamp and the wiki states the storage type, so 2^31 − 1 stands. The cap is load-bearing for the community's duplicator: multiply N by (X+1) then divide by X with X > N gives quotient N and remainder N, and "a basic duplicator can only duplicate numbers less than the square root of 2^31 - 1 (<46340)" ([Wiki Duplicator](https://beltmatic.fandom.com/wiki/Duplicator)); a level-1000 player used divmod by 45927 (9^4 × 7) and ×(6^6 + 1)/6^6 ([RdB, 2025-02-01](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2674590/discussions/0/597391520517097420/)). Zeros are made by X − X ([gotan, 2024-06-22](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2674590/discussions/0/4348869643731572205/)); players also use 1^N = 1 loops and feeding an adder with itself until 2147483647 + N = 2147483647 as number sinks ([biggieblog](https://biggieblog.com/building-number-factories-in-beltmatic/)).

What no source records: 0^0, A^0, 0^B, a negative base or exponent, and whether exponent overflow clamps like multiplication (the blog says "I also never played around with 0, negative numbers"); whether division with a negative operand floors or truncates, and the sign of R; and whether the low end saturates or wraps (only reaching −2,147,483,648 is reported). A rebuild must choose these; the safe choice consistent with everything observed is saturating arithmetic at both ends, truncating division as in C# (the engine is Unity, see below), and exponentiation by repeated multiplication with the same clamp.

## Belts carry one number a tile and junctions split round-robin

A belt tile holds one number ("Each tile holds one number", [biggieblog](https://biggieblog.com/building-number-factories-in-beltmatic/)) and advances at the belt upgrade level in tiles per second, so tiles/s equals numbers/s on a saturated belt. Belts are laid "by clicking one grid space and dragging to another"; "the start and end spaces get half-belts, and belts are skipped over blocks"; "Connecting a belt to another half-belt creates a corner" ([Supposer](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3391840619)). Placement is straight or, by default since 1.0.10, one L; Alt reverses the direction while building ([Steam news, 1.0.10](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2674590/view/755057297411015160)). Belt ends do not auto-join: "even if the connection is right it won't connect to the belt in front of it without explicit dragging" ([Steam reviews API](https://store.steampowered.com/appreviews/2674590?json=1&language=english&num_per_page=100&filter=recent)).

Junctions have four kinds and no building. "When two or more belts intersect ..., each belt at an intersection will cycle through which belt is feeding the main belt (assuming all belts have numbers waiting to move). This means that all belts have an equal distribution onto the main belt" ([Wiki Belt](https://beltmatic.fandom.com/wiki/Belt)); "Multiple inputs to a space alternate, and so do multiple outputs" ([Supposer](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3391840619)). A **corner** is the front of a new belt meeting the back of another, one continuous turning belt. A **T** is a belt running past the back end of another and "can either join two belts together, or split their contents evenly between all belts depending on the direction of the belts". A **cross made while holding Shift** "can either join three belts together, or split their contents evenly between all belts", and two opposed aligned belts meeting on a perpendicular one "will mix the contents of two belts evenly between the two". A **cross made without Shift becomes a Bridge** and "no real intersection is made" ([Wiki Belt](https://beltmatic.fandom.com/wiki/Belt)); "Bridges are now automatically built when pasting belts over existing belts. Hold shift to prevent this behaviour" ([Steam news, 1.0.7](https://steamcommunity.com/games/2674590/announcements/detail/6890025005352536761)), and "A bridge will not automatically be built if the lower belt intersects at the beginning or end of the belt being built" ([Wiki Bridge](https://beltmatic.fandom.com/wiki/Bridge)). A bridge "is just one tile large: No entry or exit - just crossing two belts" ([Steam reviews API](https://store.steampowered.com/appreviews/2674590?json=1&language=english&num_per_page=100&filter=recent)). Splits and merges carry no marker at all in the screenshots; the dark strip simply forks or joins.

**Priorities** (level 10) are the only junction control. "At any intersection, you can change the priority of the belt by left clicking on that intersection, which will apply a small green diamond. When toggling on a belt as the highest priority, any numbers flowing across it will be prioritized and any gaps in that belt will be filled by other available belts. Only one belt in an intersection can be marked as priority"; the in-game text reads "Set input/output PRIORITIES for belt intersections by clicking the belt direction. Click again to remove the priority" ([Wiki Belt](https://beltmatic.fandom.com/wiki/Belt)). Priorities work on both merges and splits ("send numbers preferentially down one exit of a junction", [Supposer](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3391840619)), which is what makes them the switch in every make-anything machine: "use the priority feature to select one digit from 0–9 ... The input must flow without gaps, or lower-priority digits can leak through" ([makki, Steam guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3270810062)). One subtlety Lord Balder had to design around: "numbers exiting a bridge always had lower priority than numbers from a belt" ([Compact MAM Designs](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2674590/discussions/0/4548155824440414415/)). Merging and splitting run at belt speed; "extractor speed upgrades are always relative to belt speed, so an integer amount of belts can be drawn from an extractor" ([Steam reviews API](https://store.steampowered.com/appreviews/2674590?json=1&language=english&num_per_page=100&filter=recent)).

Machines buffer. An operator "refuses to act if an input is over capacity, and a clogged output stalls it" ([biggieblog](https://biggieblog.com/building-number-factories-in-beltmatic/)); the Divider "must have both outputs (quotient, remainder) taken before it processes again" ([Supposer](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3391840619)). The buffer size is the second disputed figure: "Each input buffers up to 5 numbers ... an unconnected output holds 2 numbers" says the guide (updated 2025-12-16), while "A component can only hold 4 values in each input, and 2 values in each output" says the blog (2025-05-18) and "Each operator has a storage of 4 for each inputs and 2 for the output" says Lord Balder (2024-11-05), whose compact MAMs depend on the count ([biggieblog](https://biggieblog.com/building-number-factories-in-beltmatic/); [Compact MAM Designs](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2674590/discussions/0/4548155824440414415/)). **Four per input and two per output is the figure to build on**: two independent in-game observers against one guide, and the guide's other hub figure (20 belts) is also the odd one out. All three observations predate 1.0.10, whose "Various minor fixes" cannot be excluded either way.

Putting those statements together gives a simulation model that every source is consistent with, stated here as an inference because no source describes the engine's tick: belts are FIFO lanes of one item a tile advancing at `speed` tiles/s, an item moving only when the next tile is free; a junction tile with k feeding belts takes from them round-robin among those with an item ready, the priority arm first if flagged and the others filling its gaps; a tile with k outgoing belts hands items out round-robin among those with room. Operators hold an input queue of 4 a side and an output queue of 2 an output tile; an operation starts when both inputs are non-empty and the output queue has room, takes 1/speed seconds, pushes the result (or results), and outputs drain round-robin onto connected belts. Extractors emit on each connected face at belt speed up to `level` belts' worth, skipping full belts. Storage accepts and emits at belt speed on every connected face. The Hub consumes instantly, so its intake is **24 × belt speed: 24/s at L1, 48/s at L2, 96/s at L3, 192/s at L8, 480/s at L128**, the last confirmed by a level-1000 player feeding "at 480/s" from 24 belts at 20/s ([RdB, 2025-02-08](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2674590/discussions/0/597391520517097420/)). Fill times players measured: a slow design saturated its belt "100%" after 8 minutes versus 75 s for a fast one, and a later design "after like 5 numbers" ([Lord Balder](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2674590/discussions/0/4548155824440414415/)). Whether belt speed is uniform through corners, and whether the engine steps tiles discretely or moves items continuously, is not found.

## Upgrades add flat steps: big to level 8, then one or two percent a rank

"Upgrades add flat increases to speed and efficiency rather than multiplying them" ([vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/2674590/beltmatic.md)); the developer designed it so, listing "Lots of smaller upgrade levels after large upgrades" among the changes from Beltex ([pinned dev thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2674590/discussions/0/4356743320311032786/)). The developer gave the caps: "The max upgrade levels are 128 for belt, 12 for extractor and 108 for other buildings" ([Notional Games, 2024-08-27](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2674590/discussions/0/4515506995733927660/)), and a maxed player reported the end states: **Belt 128 at 20/s, Extractor 12 at 12 belts, Adder 108 at 4/s, Multiplier 108 at 3/s, Sub/Div 108 at 3/s, Exponentiator 108 at 3/s** ([neumann.emma12, 2024-09-21](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2674590/discussions/0/4338734234990223245)). There are six tracks; "The divider shares an upgrade path with the subtractor. The exponentiator has its own" ([biggieblog](https://biggieblog.com/building-number-factories-in-beltmatic/)), and the Sub/Div upgrades "are available initially at Level 13 when the Subtractor is unlocked, and begin affecting the Divider once it is unlocked at Level 16" ([Wiki Upgrades](https://beltmatic.fandom.com/wiki/Upgrades)).

The increments extrapolated to the developer's caps land exactly on the maxed player's speeds, three independent sources agreeing: 8 + 120 × 0.1 = 20, 2 + 100 × 0.02 = 4, 1 + 100 × 0.02 = 3. The closed forms are therefore **belt(L) = L for L ≤ 8, else 8 + 0.1(L − 8)**; **extractor(L) = L belts**; **adder(L) = {0.25, 0.333, 0.4, 0.5, 0.667, 1, 1.5, 2} for L ≤ 8, else 2 + 0.02(L − 8)**; and for the multiplier, sub/div and exponentiator **0.1(L + 1) for L ≤ 7, 1.0 at L8, else 1 + 0.02(L − 8)**. Two wiki infobox lines contradict the tables: the Adder infobox says ".025/s" base, a typo for 0.25 (the table row, the blog's "one addition every 4 seconds" at base and a forum quote all say 0.25), and the operator infoboxes say "+0.1/s per level" with "Level 8 speed 1/s", which is self-inconsistent (0.2 + 7 × 0.1 = 0.9); the tables' 0.8 → 1.0 step at L8 is what the capped 3/s confirms. The L4 → L5 step is confirmed in-game twice: "The speed for both is 0.5/s. The adder would upgrade to 0.667/s, but the multiplier would upgrade to 0.6/s" ([thebigh, 2024-08-05](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2674590/discussions/0/4515506834333813461/)), and the store screenshot of the Upgrades window shows Belt level 4 at 4/s with the button "CLICK TO UPGRADE TO SPEED 5/s", Extractor level 4 at 4 belts, Adder level 4 at 0.5/s, Multiplier level 4 at 0.5/s and Subtractor level 3 at 0.4/s ([ss_4](https://shared.akamai.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/2674590/ss_57ff60fafebfeac333ba81274d4bf4ce117c05df.1920x1080.jpg)). The blog's step sizes match the table to the digit: "Adder level 7 to 8 gave about a 50% boost. Adder level 8 to 9 gave about 1%" (1.5 → 2, 2 → 2.02) and an exponentiator tier "about 2%" (1 → 1.02) ([biggieblog](https://biggieblog.com/building-number-factories-in-beltmatic/)).

This table settles the belt dispute. The blog says "At base level, each belt moves 2 tiles per second" and that the hub's base maximum is 48/s, and the progression and strategy notes repeated it; the wiki table says 1/s at L1. The wiki is right: the Fast Delivery achievement, "Deliver 24 numbers per second", is described as earned "at the start by having fully saturated Level 1 Belts on every side of the main hub", with 48/s at level 2 and 96/s at level 3 ([Wiki Achievements](https://beltmatic.fandom.com/wiki/Achievements)); the ss_4 window reads level 4 as 4/s; and the first belt upgrade costs only 200 ones, so the blog's "base" is belt level 2, reached within minutes. All of the blog's throughput arithmetic (four L1 adders at 0.5 numbers/s each drink one belt, "one addition every 4 seconds") is correct for a level-2 belt. One anomaly survives: the blog also mentions a belt of **2.5 tiles/s** needing a fifth adder, a speed no level in the table produces; the notes record it without resolving it.

Each rank is paid by delivering up to three specific numbers in fixed counts. The wiki describes the structure: "Level 1: One unique number. Level 2: Two unique numbers, the unique number from Level 1 is the same, but requires more quantity. Level 3: Three unique number ... Levels 4+: A new number is added in the far right column and all numbers are 'shifted' to the left, meaning the oldest number is removed. As each number is shifted leftwards, the quantity required increases"; "There are an unlimited number of upgrades, but only the first 8 are tied to progression or achievements"; surplus carries over ("If you store 6k 75s on Adder Level 3 when only 3k were needed, once you progress to the next level, you will be 'credited' the extra 3k"); and "The actual number that is required ... is randomized within a small range ... your number may differ slightly, but the count will be the same" ([Wiki Upgrades](https://beltmatic.fandom.com/wiki/Upgrades)). The requirement list is therefore a sliding window of three over one growing sequence, which is why "delivered values can be reused for component upgrades, but only three times each" and why "At most 19 targets can be active: 3 per upgradable component and 1 for overall level" ([biggieblog](https://biggieblog.com/building-number-factories-in-beltmatic/)). The hub keeps one tally per value; the same number can be a level target and an upgrade cost at once, and "in the early game, you don't need to remove or stop delivering level numbers. In fact, early upgrades need those same numbers" ([Supposer](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3391840619)).

## The hub ladder hands out every tool by level 22, then one extractable value a level

A level is one target number and one count. The hub shows "the current number required, the current/total quantity required, as well as the next level unlocks", and the objective text reads "deliver numbers to the HUB to reach next level" ([Wiki Hub](https://beltmatic.fandom.com/wiki/Hub)). The progression note could find only the first count (ten 1s) and declared the rest not found; the wiki's Levels table, read by the machines note, has the full run to level 40 and the trailer and screenshots corroborate it: the hub reads "1 / 0/10 / LEVEL 1 / ADDER UNLOCKED NEXT LEVEL" at the start, "2 / 0/20 / LEVEL 2 / EXTRACTION 2 UNLOCKED IN 2 LEVELS" next, and "590 / 2k/4k / LEVEL 16 / DIVIDER UNLOCKED NEXT LEVEL" in the first store screenshot ([ss_0](https://shared.akamai.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/2674590/9b19409c951ef80c0553d0bb9ae293ec597cda33/ss_9b19409c951ef80c0553d0bb9ae293ec597cda33.1920x1080.jpg)), matching the table's level-16 row of ~594 × 4k. The counting convention: "Level N" is the level you are on while delivering, and its reward is held from level N + 1, which is why the hub says "next level" for level 1's adder and "in 2 levels" at level 2 for level 3's Extraction 2, and why a player list of "2 Adder, 5 Bridge, 8 Multiplier, 14 Subtractor, 17 Dividor, 20 Exponentiator, 23 Storage" ([Majibow, 2025-05-08](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2674590/discussions/0/7404569906648161108/)) is the same ladder shifted by one. The targets are "~" because they are randomised per save; the counts and rewards are fixed ([Wiki Levels](https://beltmatic.fandom.com/wiki/Levels), last edited 2024-10-29).

The wiki's "5% to 95% more" band is loose: two of these eight steps fall under 5%, so a rebuild should read the band as the envelope of a per-level range from which each target is drawn, not as a floor on consecutive steps. "Goals are not manually picked. They are random, likely within a set range per level. This applies to both the Hub's level and the upgrade tiers. The same system was used in ... Beltex"; "Goals don't grow exponentially. They appear to at first, but after reaching four digits growth visibly slows while each tier remains a larger increase than the last", with example runs "3, 14, 65" and "2057, 2803, 3917" ([Kyouko Tsukino, 2024-08-06](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2674590/discussions/0/4515506995733927660/)). The wiki quantifies the band: "The difference from one level to the next, starting by about Level 10, is between 5% more and 95% more from the previous number", and Proton measured "the average is 8.7% per level" with the warning that at 8% "the cap [2^31−1] would be reached around level 180". It is not reached: "The game never requested nine-digit numbers" and "the game didn't request >10^8" in a run to level 1000 ([RdB, 2025-02-01; IridiumMika, 2025-02-04](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2674590/discussions/0/597391520517097420/)); the blog's largest target past level 30 was 3,063,744. Continuing 8.7% from 631,448 would pass 10^8 near level 139, so **the generator must slow sharply or cap near 10^8 somewhere past level 80**; the rule is not published and is a design decision a rebuild must make. Targets "appear random, but tend toward primes" ([Kyouko, 2024-05-11](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2674590/discussions/0/4364628453800871481/)) and each level "asks for a specific number, usually prime or one or two operations away from one" ([plannedallalong](http://plannedallalong.blogspot.com/2025/08/quick-review-beltmatic.html)); the bias is claimed, never shown.

The map is "a seemingly infinite spanning grid with the Hub located at the center. Surrounding it and moving outwards are various numbers that can be extracted. The numbers start out at 1 and progressively get larger the farther away from the hub. All numbers that can be extracted will not exceed 999. There will also be no number 10's as those would make the game much easier. The map uses a seed to randomly generate the numbers and their locations" ([Wiki Map](https://beltmatic.fandom.com/wiki/Map)). Deposits are single cells, "a darkened 3×3 square around a single value" with the numeral printed in muted light blue; "The densest extractable 1s and 2s are near the hub. The nearest 11 may be about 500 tiles away" ([biggieblog](https://biggieblog.com/building-number-factories-in-beltmatic/)); the developer contrasted this with Beltex, where "Resources are extracted from a single cell instead of a patch" is listed as the change and the grid went from hex to square ([pinned dev thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2674590/discussions/0/4356743320311032786/)). The extractable set is "[1-9][11-999]" and "By the time you reach Level 30 (the end of meaningful progression), you are able to extract 23s" ([Wiki Extractor](https://beltmatic.fandom.com/wiki/Extractor)); what may be extracted is gated by level, not distance, and the deposits for locked values are already on the map (the help text says "Build an EXTRACTOR over a white '1' on the map"). The absent 10 is the game's deliberate wrinkle: the first decimal machine needs 5 + 5 or 2 × 5, and "10 must be built" ([vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/2674590/beltmatic.md)). Not found anywhere: deposit density, spacing, cluster size, the radial function mapping value to distance, whether the map is bounded, what the "obstacles" one player complained of are ([R3C0D3r, 2024-07-20](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2674590/discussions/0/4411921803479211926/)), and whether anything on the map changes after New Game. Saves live in `C:\Users\<USERNAME>\AppData\Roaming\Beltmatic\Saves` ([Notional Games, 2024-05-24](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2674590/discussions/0/4338733821686881571/)), the pause menu offers Resume, New Game, Load Game, Save Game, Options and Exit, and Steam Cloud is on; slot count, autosave and overwrite prompts are not found.

## Players factorise, build place-value machines, then wait

The strategy note's quotations are the fetch tool's renderings of pages and are close to verbatim but not guaranteed. Players converge on four methods. The commonest is to prime-factorise the target and build it from extractable values, adjusting by a small constant when the target is prime: "factor the target and look for easy combinations within about ±1-5 to use exponentiators and multipliers", about "6 operations for a 4-digit number" ([cilibrar, 2024-06-01](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2674590/discussions/0/4333105245241372162/)); 33559 = 907 × 37, and for a prime like 619 start from 61, multiply by 10 and add the rest ([destroyer156](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2674590/discussions/0/4364628453800871481/)). The second is "nearest round number plus a remainder": "10^X is your best friend once you get to that tier"; 19 = (4×4)+3; 41 = (4×9)+5; 453 = 9×50 + 3 where 50 = (5×5)+(5×5) ([Kyouko Tsukino, 2024-05-11](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2674590/discussions/0/4364628453800871481/)), and the same player finished the game having "never extracted any number bigger than 9". The third is place value: separate 10^n machines combined digit by digit, "67583 = 10^4×6 + 10^3×7 + 10^2×5 + 10×8 + 3, so one setup can make any five-digit number" ([Quazimodem](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2674590/discussions/0/4364628453800871481/)). The fourth is binary, every power of 2 summed by priority switches ([VinceG](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2674590/discussions/0/4364628453800871481/)). The blog adds single-base representation, writing the target in base 3 to 7 with balanced digits: 19553 = (((7×7+7)×7+7)×7×7×7+7+7)÷7 from eleven copies of 7, and if the target is prime "pick N near the target, build N×X, then divide" ([biggieblog](https://biggieblog.com/building-number-factories-in-beltmatic/)). Exponent-and-root tricks exist too (90182 = 18^4 − 11^4 − (14×11−1), or 2^13×11 + 5×14 "needs only four operations"), and BFS solvers for the fewest operations are widely shared, from Python scripts to beltmatic.liamk.dev and a paid iOS calculator ([Steam guides list](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2674590/guides/)). "Keep a calculator open, and perhaps a factors calculator too" is the reviewers' standing advice ([plannedallalong](http://plannedallalong.blogspot.com/2025/08/quick-review-beltmatic.html)).

Layout follows one rule: build a module once, copy-paste it along a belt. Throughput is reasoned in belts and machines per belt (four level-1 adders drink one level-2 belt, "Upgrading the extractor to two belts and copying the setup doubles throughput"), linear layouts beat "knight-move triangles" because they "copy-paste without mirroring", and the blog's author "prefers filling belts to 100%, even at the cost of extractor efficiency" ([biggieblog](https://biggieblog.com/building-number-factories-in-beltmatic/)). A typical module packs "8-15 adders/subtractors/dividers/multipliers/exponentiators into a 15x15 square area" with two input belts and one or two outputs ([cilibrar](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2674590/discussions/0/4333105245241372162/)). The endgame artefact is the make-anything machine: makki's place-value MAM keeps ten storages of the digits 0–9 (0 from X − X), makes 10 as 5 + 5 and powers of 10 with an exponentiator, selects a digit per place with priorities and sums the places in stages ([makki](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3270810062)); Lord Balder's binary MAM went through four versions, from 48 supply lines to a shift register ("about 2 minutes for the first new number and 8 minutes to saturate the belt") to a binary adder tree switched by adding or removing bridges ("about 30 s for the first number and about 75 s to fill the belt"), with switching waste cut from ~10k numbers to ~3k by replacing empty belts with zeros and adding buffer storage ([Compact MAM Designs](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2674590/discussions/0/4548155824440414415/)). Once a ten-digit MAM exists "The game becomes trivial and can be played without building anything more" ([Torin, 2024-07-30](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2674590/discussions/0/4411921803479211926/)). No guide prescribes an upgrade order; the only advice is "level them up until gains become small or they hit their level cap" ([Kyouko, 2024-06-01](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2674590/discussions/0/4333105245241372162/)), though one early snapshot had extractors maxed at 12 before any other track passed 33 ([abrid, 2024-05-27](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2674590/discussions/0/4338734234990223245)), which fits the lever that scales a copied module. No patched exploit exists; the "degenerate" strategy players name is idling.

Reception is one praise and one complaint. Praise is the number-factory idea itself (aggregate weight 1.00, "worth every cent"), accessibility next to Factorio and shapez (0.26), value (0.20), education (0.13), the UI (0.09), copy-paste and undo (0.06) and low hardware needs (0.06) ([vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/2674590/beltmatic.md)); "this is a godsend to the autistic community (me)" has 194 helpful votes ([Steam reviews, top rated](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2674590/reviews?browsefilter=toprated)). The complaint is that it goes flat: "repetitive and shallow" (0.46, "becomes idle or repetitive, with weak endgame content"), limited replayability (0.12), missing QoL such as a speed-up (0.11), inferior to shapez (0.09), unrewarding upgrades (0.05) and a near-useless divider (0.03). Representative negatives: "once you unlock all the combinators it starts to feel very same-y" (160 helpful) and "It very quickly turns into an idle game that you leave running in the background ... you cannot mirror/flip designs" (56 helpful). Requested and never delivered: mirror and flip, a blueprint library, sandbox and challenge maps, a speed-up, decimals and negatives as goals, logic gates, macOS, Chinese; delivered on request: undo/redo, auto-bridges on paste, L-shaped belts and Alt to reverse. shapez is the comparison in nearly every review ("Shapez, with numbers"); Beltex is the predecessor it improves on with the square grid, priorities and a faster start; nobody mentions Mindustry or shapez 2.

## Conclusion

The game is far smaller than its forum suggests: nine building types, six additive upgrade tracks whose entire shape is a hand-tuned eight-step head followed by a hundred ranks of +0.02, one per-value delivery ledger that serves both levels and upgrades, and a target generator that is the only thing the developer never exposed. Everything a rebuild needs to feel like Beltmatic is in the tables above; the three decisions it must make for itself are the exponent and signed-division edge cases, the deposit layout, and the late target curve, where the honest constraint is "about 8.7% a level in the seventies, never past 10^8 by level 1000". The disputes in the notes all resolved the same way: the wiki's tables and the in-game observers beat the guide and the blog's untested "base" figures, which is a useful rule for any further research on this game.

Two things the reception data imply for a successor. First, the curve the developer chose, big steps to rank 8 and crumbs after, is exactly what players call "unrewarding", yet it is also what makes "Reasonably Upgraded" a clean finish line that 6% reach; a rebuild that wants a longer game should extend the head of the curve, not the tail. Second, the absent 10, the integer clamp and the unemitted zero remainder are not bugs to fix but the three rules the community's cleverest builds (duplicators, sequencers, MAMs) are made of; drop any of them and the strategy layer above collapses.
