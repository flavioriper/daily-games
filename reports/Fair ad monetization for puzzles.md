# Move the ad after the puzzle, not before

The profitable, non-abusive plan for this game is **rewarded-first**: opt-in videos for a fourth hint, a doubled Arcade payout and a continue after an Arcade loss. Add **a capped interstitial only after a game ends, never before one starts**, keep the banner, and keep gold earned-only. Of the three proposals, (2) is sound and matches what the best-performing puzzle placements look like. (1) has the right instinct and the wrong mechanics. Its placement, "before the game starts", is the one spot Google Play's Ads policy names as not allowed ("ads that appear during game play at the beginning of a level"). Its 15-20 free games a day is so generous that almost nobody would see an ad, because typical players run 3-5 sessions a day. (3) should be dropped as written. A second, purchasable coin next to earned gold is the "mixing different in-game virtual currencies" pattern that EU consumer authorities tell games to avoid. It would bring real-money price disclosure, parental purchase controls under Brazil's ECA Digital and the "pay to win" label, which is the top reason players give for quitting a game. Its good half, a watch-an-ad fallback after a loss, belongs in the rewarded plan instead. The stakes are real: a banner-only game earns almost nothing, especially in Brazil, where a banner impression pays about **US$0.10 per thousand**, against roughly $1.90-3.75 for a rewarded view. A separate compliance gap matters more than any placement decision. The game has no age gate and sends ad requests with no child or under-age tags, in a cozy art style that makes it plausibly "of probable access" by minors under Brazilian law.

A note on evidence. The binding sources here are platform policy (Google Play, AdMob, Apple) and statute. The retention evidence is thin: one peer-reviewed observational study, and one large survey commissioned by Google. Most concrete numbers for cooldowns and caps come from ad-network or ad-vendor blogs, and they are flagged as such below. Wherever this report reasons from those sources to this game, it says it is inferring.

## "Before the game starts" is the one placement Google names as banned

**Evidence.** Google Play's Ads policy is explicit: "Ads that appear during game play at the beginning of a level or during the beginning of a content segment are not allowed," and full-screen ads that "show unexpectedly, typically when the user has chosen to do something else" are banned ([Google Play Ads policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9857753)). The Better Ads Experiences page defines "unexpected" as the moment when "users clicked on a button expecting to start their user journey, rather than see an ad." It lists the end of a level, the end of a content section and after score screens as the acceptable slots ([Google Play Better Ads Experiences](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12271244)). AdMob adds its own floor: no interstitials on app load or exit, none after every user action (at most one per two actions), none back to back, and none that "suddenly appear when a user is focused on a task at hand" ([AdMob disallowed interstitial implementations](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/6201362)). Google Play also allows an ad placed "after the score screen in a game app" to run longer than the usual 15-second close limit, which shows where Google considers a full-screen ad non-interrupting ([Google Play Ads policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9857753)).

The churn evidence points the same way. The largest recent study is Deloitte's survey for Google AdMob of 7,000 gamers, commissioned by Google, so an interested party. It found that one exposure to disruptive ad features raises churn 6-7%, that **quit rates triple when disruptive ads hit end-cards or reward screens**, that casual gamers are 30-50% more likely to churn after them, and that repeated exposure pushes 52% to quit ([PR Newswire, Deloitte/AdMob](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/growth-in-mobile-gaming-new-global-study-from-deloitte-and-google-admob-reveals-the-link-between-mobile-ad-engagement-and-gamer-retention-302477095.html)). An older study of user-level data from 21 free-to-play games found only a "weak, insignificant effect" of ad count on retention, with game quality dominating ([Burns, Roseboom & Ross, AAAI 2016](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/12906/12906-52-16423-1-2-20201228.pdf)). Taken together: *how disruptive* an ad is matters more than *how many* ads there are.

**Inference for this game.** The daily streak runs through the moment the player taps a card, and three boards a day keep it. An ad at that moment breaks policy and lands at the point of highest intent, every day, on the retention hook itself. The fix keeps the business logic and moves the ad. The slot is the player dismissing the win screen and returning to the grid. It should not cover the win celebration, which is the "reward screen" Deloitte flags. Ad-network tests also report that small reorderings around the end of a level move revenue: +24% interstitial revenue with "no retention decline reported" in one 2025 case ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/what-five-in-game-ad-experiments-taught-us-about-player-behaviour-and-revenue/)), and +5.5% ARPU in Supersonic's hyper-casual tests ([Supersonic](https://supersonic.com/learn/blog/5-a-b-tests-you-should-be-running-to-improve-your-hyper-casual-game/)). Neither published retention curves, and no source quantified before-level against after-level retention directly.

## A 15-20 game daily quota protects almost everyone and earns almost nothing

**Evidence.** No shipped game or published case uses a per-day ad-free quota. Industry grace periods are per install (the first ad at level 3-8 in hyper-casual; "at least two weeks" for casual and midcore, per ironSource) or per session (the first 60-90 seconds) ([Unity/ironSource](https://unity.com/blog/best-practices-for-maximizing-revenue-from-interstitial-ads-in-your-app); [Felix Braberg](https://felixbraberg.substack.com/p/7-best-practices-for-interstitial)). GameAnalytics' Q1 2024 benchmarks across more than 10,000 games put typical play at **3-5 sessions a day with a 4.45-minute median session** ([GameAnalytics benchmarks](https://gameindustrylibrary.com/documents/q1-mobile-games-benchmarks-2024/read)). Shipped ad-led puzzle hits are far harsher. Words of Wonders shows interstitials after every level from level 8, and Vita Mahjong "after nearly every level" ([Felix Braberg #26](https://felixbraberg.substack.com/p/the-top-6-android-games-making-a)). Those are level-grinders, not a cozy daily game. The published cooldown figures are mostly vendor opinion without data: 60-90 s before a session's first ad, 2-3 minutes between ads, and 4-6 per session ([AdReact](https://adreact.com/blog/interstitial-ad-best-practices-mobile-games/)). The one platform-enforced minimum found is Meta Instant Games' 30 s ([Meta for Developers](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/games/monetize/in-app-ads/interstitial-ads)).

**Inference.** At 15-20 free games a day, the interstitial reaches only a small tail of heavy players. Those are the players most likely to buy remove-ads or stay for years, and the ones you least want to annoy. It resets daily, so those players meet the same wall every day, while new players, whom the published advice concentrates on protecting, are protected no better. A count also treats a 20-second board, a 10-minute snooker frame and an Arcade run as equal units. "Every 2nd game" without a time floor could fire two ads a minute on fast boards. The standard fix is a **dual cap**: at least N games *and* at least M minutes since the last ad. Add a **per-install** grace period and reset the timer after any rewarded view, so an interstitial never follows a video ([Felix Braberg](https://felixbraberg.substack.com/p/7-best-practices-for-interstitial)). The right threshold is an empirical question that the project's own analytics can answer better than any public benchmark. The distribution of `puzzle_complete` per user per day shows exactly how many players a given quota exempts.

The business case for *some* interstitial is real. Where puzzle games use them, interstitials are usually the largest ad line. Vita Mahjong is estimated at about **70% of ad revenue** from interstitials ([Felix Braberg #26](https://felixbraberg.substack.com/p/the-top-6-android-games-making-a)). A rewarded-heavy casual game optimised the other way saw interstitials fall to 17% ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/what-five-in-game-ad-experiments-taught-us-about-player-behaviour-and-revenue/)). That second case is the model that fits a cozy brand.

## Rewarded video is the right engine, and proposal 2 already points at it

**Evidence.** Unity's 2024 report (2023 data) found **38.4% of daily players in word games and 36% in casual games** engage with rewarded video. **Context-sensitive placements, such as running out of resources, drew 38.1% engagement against 23.8% for placements between levels** ([PocketGamer.biz on Unity's report](http://www.pocketgamer.biz/unity-global-rewarded-ad-engagement-rose-by-32-in-2023/)). Unity ranks the top puzzle placements as reward doubling, capped extra currency, a revive on loss, boosters, and extra lives or spin wheels ([Unity: top 5 rewarded placements for puzzle](https://unity.com/kr/blog/top-5-rewarded-video-placements-to-boost-puzzle-game-revenue)). Block Blast, the top-earning ad-funded game (about $127M in ad revenue in January-May 2026), reportedly runs **one rewarded placement, a revive at the failure state** ([Applixir, vendor analysis](https://www.applixir.com/blog/the-block-blast-playbook-what-127m-in-ad-revenue-actually-bought/)). Rewarded ads pay the most in every region. Appodeal's Q4 2024 data puts rewarded at $9.20 (Android) and $13.90 (iOS) in North America and **$1.90/$3.75 in Latin America**, against banners at **$0.55/$0.35 and $0.10/$0.10** ([Mistplay summary of Appodeal](https://business.mistplay.com/resources/mobile-ads-ecpm/)). Players dislike rewarded video least. Older surveys have 46% preferring it to other formats ([GamesBeat on Unity](https://gamesbeat.com/unity-the-majority-of-mobile-gamers-are-open-to-engaging-with-incentivized-video-ads/)), and ironSource reports that rewarded ads and in-app purchases "do not cannibalize each other" ([ironSource 2022 summary](https://gameindustrylibrary.com/documents/ironsource-modern-mobile-consumer-2022-app-monetization-report-2022)). An industry panel warns that cannibalisation is neither always nor never true and depends on the economy's balance ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/5-common-mobile-advertising-myths/)).

The policy conditions are simple and strict. The player must "affirmatively and unambiguously" opt in. The action and the reward must be disclosed before each instance. The reward must always be delivered. The player can decline with no penalty. No real-money reward is allowed ([AdMob rewarded ads policy](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/7313578)). On iOS, a reward can never depend on granting App Tracking Transparency ([App Review Guidelines 5.1.2(i)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)).

**Inference, checked against the code.** Every daily board today gives **three free hints (two on Hidden Word)**, set by each board's `HINTS` constant, and nothing sells or gates them. So "one more hint" means a fourth hint for a video once the free ones run out. That is a textbook context-sensitive placement, and it adds a door without taking anything away. It must stay a *fourth* hint: cutting the free three to create scarcity for the ad would turn a courtesy into "pay to skip," a named monetary dark pattern ([Zagal et al., DiGRA 2020](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/156460/1/DiGRA_2020_paper_189.pdf)). The Arcade doubler is Unity's top puzzle placement. Offered on the end card, though, it sits in the weaker "between levels" group, so expect lower take-up than for the hint or the continue. It should double *gold*, never the score, which matches the existing rule that boosters never multiply a score. It should also respect the wallet's daily Arcade cap, or the cap stops meaning anything. Rewarded "mystery box" and spin-wheel placements should stay out: they are unpaid, but random, and the game's current "nothing is random" rule is its cleanest defence under Brazil's loot-box ban.

## A second, buyable coin brings the rules the game currently avoids

**Evidence.** The EU's CPC Network Key Principles (March 2025; not binding, but built on the unfair-commercial-practices and consumer-rights directives) explicitly **exclude currency "that can solely be obtained through gameplay."** Once a currency is bought, though, it must show a real-money price beside every item, avoid "mixing different in-game virtual currencies in one video game," avoid bundles that do not match item prices, and respect the 14-day withdrawal right. For any game "not exclusively intended for an adult audience," the principles also say to consider **disabling real-money spending by default** ([CPC Key Principles PDF](https://esportslegal.news/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Key-principles-on-in-game-virtual-currencies.pdf)). Brazil's ECA Digital (Lei 15.211/2025) has been in force since 17 March 2026. It bans paid random rewards in games "of probable access" by minors ([Lei 15.211 cap. VII](https://www.vademecumprevidenciario.com.br/legislacao/nivel/lei_00152112025/capitulo-vii-dos-jogos-eletronicos)). According to a games law firm, it also requires reliable age verification, protective defaults and parental tools to "restrict purchases," with fines up to R$50 million or 10% of revenue ([Caputo Duarte](https://caputoduarte.com.br/lei-felca-eca-digital-15-211-2025-novas-obrigacoes-e-impactos-praticos-para-estudios-de-jogos-brasileiros/)); those article numbers were not checked against the official text. Enforcement is active: in June 2026 a Brazilian court fined a Riot Games subsidiary R$15 million over paid random rewards to children ([TJDFT](https://www.tjdft.jus.br/institucional/imprensa/noticias/2026/junho/justica-condena-empresa-de-jogos-eletronicos-por-pratica-abusiva-com-criancas-e-adolescentes-em-recompensas-pagas)). The FTC's $20 million HoYoverse order treated obscured virtual-currency pricing as deceptive ([FTC](https://ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2025/01/level-tips-businesses-ftcs-settlement-genshin-impact-developer-hoyoverse)). In a 2023 Mistplay survey, "pay to win" was the **top reason players quit a game (59.75%)**, and only 28.64% of players wanted flexible in-game currency in offers against 45.95% who preferred one-time deals ([GameDev Reports on Mistplay](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/mistplay-north-american-players-behaviour)). The respected daily-puzzle brands (NYT Games, Puzzmo, Knotwords, Good Sudoku) sell content, archives, statistics and no ads, never advantage ([ADN on Puzzmo](https://www.adn.com/puzzles/2024/06/08/play-traditional-newspaper-games-with-a-new-spin/); [Six Colors on Knotwords](https://sixcolors.com/post/2022/04/knotwords-offers-crossword-puzzles-without-clues/)).

**Inference.** A non-random premium coin is not illegal. But it moves the game out of the "earned-only" exemption and into a set of obligations: price labels, a single currency, withdrawal information, and parental purchase controls in Brazil. The last of these is unresolved for a game with no age gate. It also buys a label, "pay to win," that undercuts the cozy daily positioning. Selling hints that touch the daily boards would mean paid help on a streak and on the shared crowd comparisons, which is where perceived unfairness concentrates. The "only after a loss" framing makes it worse. Dark-pattern research ties loss-time offers to loss aversion and "endowed value" ([arXiv 2412.05039](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.05039)), and consumer rules target practices that exploit vulnerability or urge children to buy (CPC Principle 7; Brazil's CDC art. 37 §2). A *rewarded-video* continue at that moment costs the player no money and is standard. A *paid* one at the frustration moment, to an audience that may include children, is the riskiest configuration in this whole analysis. No regulator text found calls a paid continue after a loss unlawful as such; that risk is inferred from the general rules. If paid items are ever wanted, sell the *same* gold with its real-money price shown, never a second coin, or better, sell a one-time supporter product.

## Remove-ads is underpriced for what it would have to cover

**Evidence.** Comparable remove-ads purchases cost **$9.99-$14.99** (Sudoku.com $14.99; Words of Wonders about $9.99), often beside weekly VIP plans at $3.99-$4.49 that add hints and daily currency ([Sudoku.com App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/sudoku-com-number-games/id1193508329?xs=1); [Words of Wonders App Store](https://apps.apple.com/JP/app/id1369521645)). Reportedly, Block Blast's subscription removes interstitials but keeps rewarded ads ([Udonis](https://www.blog.udonis.co/statistics/block-blast)); another source disputes that Block Blast sells remove-ads at all ([Applixir](https://www.applixir.com/blog/the-block-blast-playbook-what-127m-in-ad-revenue-actually-bought/)). In Mistplay's 2023 North American survey, 29.27% of spenders named ad-free play as a reason they pay ([GameDev Reports on Mistplay](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/mistplay-north-american-players-behaviour)).

**Inference.** At US$4.99 / R$19.99, the current price is fair for hiding a banner that earns about US$0.0015 per player per day in Brazil. Once interstitials exist, the purchase is worth more and can carry a higher price. Existing owners must keep everything they bought. Remove-ads should remove the banner and every interstitial. It should *not* remove the rewarded offers, since they are optional free rewards. Instead it can **grant their rewards without the video** within the same caps. That gives the lifetime purchase a reason to exist in markets where the banner alone is nearly worthless. No published conversion rate for remove-ads was found; overall F2P payer conversion is commonly quoted at 2-5% ([Business of Apps](https://www.businessofapps.com/data/mobile-game-conversion-rates/)).

### What the money looks like

The following is rough arithmetic from benchmark eCPMs (Appodeal Q4 2024 via [Mistplay](https://business.mistplay.com/resources/mobile-ads-ecpm/); Brazil interstitial $2.09-3.67 per [MonetizeMore](https://www.monetizemore.com/blog/ecpm-insights/)). It assumes 15 banner impressions per player per day, 0.3-0.8 rewarded views and 1-2 interstitials. AdMob-only fill on a new app usually comes in below these network averages.

| Per 1,000 daily players, per day | US | Latin America / Brazil |
|---|---|---|
| Banner only (today) | ~$7.50 | ~$1.50 |
| Add rewarded (hint, doubler, continue) | +$4.50-12 | +$0.60-3 |
| Add one capped end-of-game interstitial | +$13-25 at 1-2/day | +$2-7 at 1-2/day |

The rewarded layer alone plausibly doubles banner revenue with little retention risk. The interstitial is the biggest line, and also the one that has to be earned with careful placement.

## The concrete ad plan

The plan below keeps the daily core pure, puts the money in opt-in moments, and adds one polite interstitial. Every number should be a remotely switchable setting, and the numbers are starting points to test rather than findings.

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Banner** | Keep the anchored banner on the menu and tabs. Never show it on the win screen's celebration. |
| **Interstitial: placement** | Only when the player leaves a finished game (win screen dismissed, Arcade/Versus end card closed, "back to grid"). Never on tapping a card, on app open or resume, over a win celebration, or on Hidden Word's loss reveal. Preload it, and skip the slot if it is not already loaded. |
| **Interstitial: grace** | None in the first 3 days after install. **None until the day's three hearts are earned** (the streak path stays ad-free). Start at "after 6 finished games today" and tune it from the analytics distribution, not 15-20. |
| **Interstitial: caps** | At least 2 finished games **and** at least 4 minutes since the last full-screen ad, whichever comes later; at most 4 a day; never within 4 minutes after a rewarded view. Prefer static or skippable creatives, and avoid 30-second unskippable video, the format least in keeping with a cozy game. |
| **Rewarded: fourth hint** | After a board's free hints are used, a "Watch a video for one more hint" button that states the reward. One a board. Never cut the free hints. |
| **Rewarded: Arcade doubler** | On the Arcade end card: "Watch to double this run's gold." Doubles gold, never score. Counts within the daily Arcade gold cap. |
| **Rewarded: continue** | On an Arcade loss, alongside Second chance: "spend gold **or** watch a video." One continue a run, no countdown, no escalating offers. Mark the run like a boosted best. |
| **Rewarded: daily cap** | About 8-10 videos a day across all placements (vendor range 10-20; stay lower for the brand). |
| **Remove-ads covers** | The banner and all interstitials. Rewarded rewards granted instantly without the video, within the same caps. Consider a higher price for new buyers once interstitials ship; existing owners keep everything. |
| **Stays pure** | The daily boards' free hints, the streak, the hearts, the crowd comparisons, and scores. No paid currency, no random rewards, no paid continue. Gold stays earned-only. |
| **Compliance, before any of this** | Set a maximum ad-content rating (G or PG) on requests. Serve non-personalised ads to Brazilian users, or add a neutral age screen that sends under-13s as child-directed and 13-17s tagged as under-age. Decide the Play target audience honestly (under-13 brings the Families rules: no ad at launch, rewarded closeable after 5 s). Stay out of Apple's Kids Category. |

The compliance row describes the current state of the code. `core/ads.gd` sets no child-directed flag, no under-age tag and no maximum content rating today; it relies on UMP consent alone. Under the ECA Digital's ban on profiling-based ads to minors ([TechTudo](https://www.techtudo.com.br/guia/2026/03/eca-digital-entenda-como-a-lei-felca-pode-afetar-os-games-no-brasil-edjogos.ghtml)), the updated COPPA rule (compliance required by 22 April 2026, with new standards for mixed-audience services) ([BBB National Programs](https://bbbprograms.org/media/insights/blog/coppa-amended)) and Google Play's Families rules ([Play Families](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335)), this gap carries more legal risk than any of the three proposals.

**What to measure.** First, pull what already exists. The distribution of `puzzle_complete` events per user per day, split by `locale`, sets the interstitial threshold from real behaviour. `puzzle_abandon`, `hearts` and `streak` on `puzzle_complete`, and `arcade_end` against `arcade_abandon` are the guardrails. `store_opened`, with its `door`, and `purchase_complete` show whether ads push players toward remove-ads. Then add an event for each ad lifecycle step, tagged with a `placement` parameter: `ad_rewarded_offered`, `ad_rewarded_started`, `ad_rewarded_completed`, `ad_interstitial_shown` and `ad_interstitial_skipped_not_loaded`. Opt-in rate is completed over offered; vendors treat below about 30% as a weak offer ([Applixir, vendor](https://www.applixir.com/?p=1792)). Run each change as a two-week holdout, as ironSource advises. Judge it on D1/D7 retention and three-heart days per active player, not on ad revenue alone. Cut the interstitial frequency if D1 falls without a proportional revenue gain ([Unity/ironSource](https://unity.com/blog/best-practices-for-maximizing-revenue-from-interstitial-ads-in-your-app)).

## Conclusion

The developer's instincts were half right. A generous grace period and a watch-an-ad fallback on loss both point at the fair version of this business. The mechanics were the problem: an ad at the moment of intent, a quota counted per day instead of per install and per minute, and a currency sale that would pull the game into the regulatory and reputational category it has so far stayed out of. The deeper takeaway is that this game's value is its *daily ritual*. The money should come from moments the player has already chosen: asking for help, finishing a run, losing one. It should not come from the doorway to the next puzzle.

Two things remain unknown and should be settled by the game's own data rather than by blogs. The first is how many games a day real players finish, which decides whether any interstitial is worth its churn at all. The second is whether AdMob-only fill in Brazil and Spanish-speaking Latin America comes anywhere near the network averages above. If the rewarded layer alone moves revenue enough, the interstitial may never need to ship, and a rewarded-plus-banner design is the position most defensible to players, to Google and to Brazilian regulators.
