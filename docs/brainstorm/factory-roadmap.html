Brainstorm · 2026-10-10Factory, the long road

A calm build-and-sell game on a board of plates. It replaces the Valley tab: one game, one money, one screen, nothing split into places. The player chops by hand, lights a fire, automates, and ends up somewhere futuristic, selling everything along the way to buy the next module.

Working name: Factorysixth tab, in place of Valley
fixed isometric view, no rotation

What it is

Farm, factory, sell. Trees and rocks on a plate of land; belts carry what comes off them to a market; the market sells so many items a second; the coins buy fires, kilns, more plates. Every tier a thing passes through makes it worth more, and the only way to earn is to sell.

Every number of mass, heat and power is real and lives in one place, the physics ledger (docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-10-factory-physics-design.md): the game runs at 4x, a log holds a log's heat, a steam engine wastes what one wastes, a tile of solar gives a tile's sun. The first build is 2026-10-10-factory-board-and-hand-era-design.md.

Two games are the references and neither may be named in code, comments, commits or on screen, by the renamed-genre rule: the browser belt game Conveyor Idle gave the loop and the plate board; the PC factory game Satisfactory gave the long arc of power and overload. That is the only time this page names them. In the PC game the player grows the base; here the player sells everything and buys modules, like an incremental.

One moneyCoins from the market buy everything. No gems, no premium counter, no prestige in the first spec (the economy leaves room for one later).
Nothing while closedThe land runs only while the app is open. No away earnings, no "come back" card, no doubling ad.
No hurry, no anxietyNothing times out, spills, breaks or is lost. A tap is labour in the hand era, never a hurry on a machine. Over demand everything just runs slower.
Slow and endlessThe land moves only during a session and a session is as long as the player wants. It is never done, and numbers are not made faster for a demo.
Fixed isometricThe Grove's view, corner on, no camera rotation. The board pans by drag.
Any ageComfortable and lovely for anyone: the palette is pictures, prices are coins, the tutorial is the first three taps.

The board of plates

The land is not one grid that grows at its edges; it is plates, bought one at a time, each given a kind by the player, joined like a board game. Belts run across the seam between two plates as if it were not there.

A plate's price climbs with the count owned, not with its kind, so the choice of kind is the player's taste and plan, never a price. Only kinds the current era has unlocked are offered. A plate is never sold back: the land only grows.

The road, era by era

Each era opens on one purchase (a plate for the second, a key module for the rest) and adds exactly one new complexity layer, one new power source and one new product tier. The player can stay in any era as long as they like; nothing nags.

By hand era 1

From the first open. Ends when the second plate is bought. About twenty-five minutes of play on a four-minute regrow, longer if the player potters.

Board

  • One forest plate, the market standing on its front corner from the start
  • Six trees up, ten at most; a felled tree regrows from its stump in four minutes

Labour

  • Tap a tree: the beaver comes, bites three times, the tree comes down the Grove's way, a log lies on the tile at its foot
  • Rocks come with a quarry plate: a tap chips a stone off, the next chip is ready three minutes later; a rock never runs out
  • A log or a stone lies on the tile where it fell and rides the first belt laid on that tile; nothing is dragged by hand

Modules

  • Belt, 1 coin a tile, a tile in two seconds, two items a tile
  • Market, given, sells one item every four seconds, pays the item's price; its levels raise the rate

Products

  • Log 1 coin · Stone 2 coins
Lesson: things ride belts, a laid belt is a decision, and the plate you buy next chooses what the next twenty minutes are about.

Fire era 2

Opens with the second plate. Ends when the Stoker is bought. Fire alone gives heat, not turning power, so the machines here are the ones that want heat; the saw waits for steam.

Board

  • The second plate: quarry or meadow, the player's pick

Power, tended by hand

  • The Firebox sits beside a machine. Logs reach it by belt and pile in its hopper, five at most
  • It burns only while someone keeps it lit: a tap puts one log on; with a kiln working it lasts twenty minutes, then dims to embers. The kiln pauses, calm, until the next tap. Nothing is lost and no log is wasted
  • One firebox heats every machine touching it, sharing its heat; a second machine wants a second fire

Modules

  • Firebox
  • Kiln: stone in, lime out, needs heat, four minutes a stone
  • Retort: log in, charcoal out, needs heat; charcoal is the smelter's fuel later

Products

  • Lime 10 · Charcoal 4
Lesson: machines want power, a fire has a draw, and someone has to light it. The player feels the chore just long enough to want a stoker.

Steam era 3

Opens with the Stoker. Ends when the Water Wheel is bought. Heat becomes turning power: a boiler and engine on a firebox, 7.5 kW of shaft from 94 of fire, as honest as a small mill engine.

Automation

  • Stoker: fitted to a firebox, it feeds the fire from the hopper by itself
  • Boiler and engine: sits on a firebox, gives shaft power to what touches it and along a shaft tile laid like a belt
  • Saw: log in, four planks out; Stonecutter, Joiner (three planks, a chair), Lathe
  • Lodge: the Grove's own beavers chop every tree in its reach and lay the logs on its belt
  • Drill: digs stone from a quarry plate without a tap, and coal where a seam is; wants a whole engine's shaft
  • Coal from the drill burns longest; the firebox's levels raise its heat

Power, the first limit

  • Heat reaches what touches the fire; shaft reaches what touches the engine or a shaft tile. No wires yet; a meter a kind, supply over demand
  • Over demand, every machine on that source runs at supply over demand. Nothing stops, nothing trips
  • A belt carries an item a second; the fast belt two. A full belt waits, it never spills
  • Splitter: one belt into two, alternating

Products

  • Plank 2 · Block 5 · Coal 3 · Chair 12
Lesson: automation is bounded. Power and belt capacity are the two numbers the player plans around for the rest of the game.

Water and wind era 4

Opens with the Water Wheel. Ends when the Dynamo is bought.

Board

  • Pond and hill plates unlock

Power, clean but placed

  • Water wheel: only on a pond plate's rim, steady power with no fuel
  • Windmill: only on a hill plate, steady power with no fuel
  • Limited by where they fit, so the player buys plates for power, not just for ore

Modules

  • Smelter: ore and charcoal in, an ingot out, eight minutes each; the hungriest thing on the land
  • Nailer: one ingot in, two boxes of nails out
  • The Joiner takes two inputs now: planks and nails make a table
  • Merger: two belts into one

Products

  • Ingot 10 · Nails 8 (two a box an ingot) · Table 30
Lesson: two inputs. Belts merge, split and cross plates, and the board starts to look like a factory.

Electric era 5

Opens with the Dynamo. Ends when the Solar Panel is bought.

Board

  • Shore plates unlock; a second market may be bought

Power

  • Dynamo: burns anything, coal best, big supply
  • Machines of this era draw more than all of era 3 together

Modules

  • Bridge: a belt over a belt
  • Glassworks: sand in, glass out
  • Assembler: three inputs
  • Market rate upgrades climb through here

Products

  • Glass 15 · Lamp (glass, ingot) 60 · Bicycle (ingots, nails, planks) 150
Lesson: crossing belts and three-input recipes. Layout becomes the game.

Solar era 6

Opens with the Solar Panel. Ends when the Core is bought.

Power

  • Solar panel: fits anywhere, no fuel, a fifth of a kilowatt on average a tile and nothing at night: a plate of them to run one saw
  • Battery: holds a surplus so a hungry machine can draw in bursts

Modules

  • Drone pad: two pads carry items between them with no belt, across any distance
  • Circuit press: glass and ingot make a circuit
  • Radio works: a circuit and glass make a radio
  • Robot works: circuits, ingots and lamps make a robot

Products

  • Circuit 80 · Radio 250 · Robot 800
Lesson: belts are no longer the only way to move a thing, and a big board stays readable.

Beyond era 7

Opens with the Core. Never ends.

Power

  • Core: one tile, power for a whole land

Modules

  • Printer: any product from a pulp of anything, at the printer's level
  • Pad: an item that steps on it steps off any other pad
  • Every module gains levels with no cap; price is the brake, as in Peapod

Products

  • The printer's tiers climb forever, each a round number above the last
Lesson: there is no end. A land here is a garden the player tends, and the numbers are the only thing that grows.

The economy's skeleton

Prices are the game's and a first guess, to be tuned by a pace bot, never by a demo; rates are the ledger's and are not tuned. What is fixed is the shape: a product is worth about four times what went into it, a module costs about five times the one before, and a plate's price triples with each owned.

What sells back

A module sold back returns its full price. The game is about selling, and a player who tries a layout and undoes it loses nothing but the minute. Plates alone are kept. Items on a belt that is sold back lie on their tiles and ride the first belt laid on those tiles, as in era 1.

Prestige, left room for

Not in the first spec. If the numbers at era 7 need a second axis, it is a sell the whole land that keeps a multiplier on prices and starts a new first plate. The economy above is built so that a multiplier on prices alone would carry it; nothing else should be made to depend on a second currency.

How it is played on a phone

The screen

  • The board takes the screen; the coin count sits on a pill at the top
  • The palette is a drawer on the left (the thumb that builds rests on the right; the thumb-side rule)
  • The power meter appears from era 3 as a thin bar beside the coins: supply over demand, green to amber, never red

Building

  • Pick a module in the drawer, tap a tile: it stands, the price leaves the pill
  • Belt picked, drag across tiles: a belt is laid along the drag in its direction; lift to end
  • Tap a placed belt to turn it a quarter; tap a machine to open its card (what it makes, its level, sell back)
  • Long press a module: it is sold back
  • Drag on open ground pans

Era 1 labour

  • Tap a tree: the beaver bites; tap a rock: a chip; tap a cold firebox: it lights
  • From era 3 these taps still work but nobody needs them

What never happens

  • No countdown, no decay, no spoilage, no blackout
  • No away earnings, no ad for a boost, no second currency
  • No camera rotation

What it costs to draw

This is the heaviest thing the project will have drawn: plates, belts, items riding them, machines, trees and beavers in one view, under a budget of 855 draw calls on gl_compatibility.

  • A plate is one mesh, the Grove's ground, and never redrawn while it stands.
  • Belts are one MultiMesh for the whole land, a tile an instance, the arrows a scrolling UV in the material; a belt laid or turned rebuilds the buffer, not a mesh a tile.
  • Items are one MultiMesh a kind: a log is an instance, never a draw. Ten kinds on screen is ten draws for any number of items.
  • A machine is one cached mesh a kind drawn under a transform; its moving part (a blade, a flame, a wheel) is a second instance of a shared mesh.
  • Trees and beavers stay as the Grove drew them: the Grove's late land measured 379 under the oval, which is the known floor to build on.
  • No instance uniform anywhere; a per-instance colour goes in the MultiMesh's colour. Checked on opengl3_angle before it ships.

In what order it gets built

The first Godot spec is eras 1 and 2 with the plate board, the tab swap and the tutorial: enough to replace the Valley tab with something that plays. Each later era is a pass with its own spec, the way Peapod and Nightlight grew.

  1. The physics ledger, written: every source, sink, item and module of every era with its honest basis, so no later era contradicts an earlier one.
  2. Spec one: the board and the hand era, written. Plates, the forest and quarry and meadow kinds, belts, the market and its rate, trees and rocks tapped, the firebox tended by hand, the kiln and the retort. The tab swap, save, tutorial, the pace bot. The Grove's code goes in this pass.
  3. Era 3, Steam. The boiler and engine, shaft tiles, the saw and its kin, the stoker, the lodge, the drill, coal, the fast belt, the splitter, the meters.
  4. Era 4, Water and wind. Pond and hill plates, wheels, windmills, iron, two-input recipes, the merger.
  5. Era 5, Electric. Bridges, glass, the assembler, the second market.
  6. Era 6 and 7. Solar, batteries, drones, the core, printers, pads.
  7. Sounds, haptics, pt and es follow each pass under the cozy rules: a tap on a tree is the Grove's dry tick, a belt is silent, a sale is a low tock.

The playable concept tab in docs/brainstorm/concepts.html comes from spec one before Godot code, by the standing rule for a new game.

Open calls

Decided in this brainstorm, and what is still yours to call.

  • decided Free grid with hand-laid belts; items are the only money, sold through a market with a rate; one money; nothing while closed; no hurry taps; fixed isometric; the Grove dropped, its art reused; plates bought on an edge with a kind the player picks.
  • assumed The fire is tended by tapping it, one fuel item a tap, twenty minutes a log under a kiln, the kiln pausing dim in between. If "manually feed" meant something else, say so.
  • assumed Belts exist from the first tap: a log lies where the tree fell and rides the first belt laid against it. The alternative, a hand era with no belts where the log walks to the market by itself, teaches belts later and was not chosen.
  • your call The name. Factory is the working name. Candidates with the same warmth as the other tabs: Workshop, Millbrook, Tinker, Hollow Mill.
  • your call A plate is 8 by 8. It fits the Grove's diamond at the size the trees are drawn; 10 by 10 would make them small.
  • your call Sell back at full price. Chosen for calm; a 20 percent loss would make a layout a commitment.
  • your call Power as one land-wide meter with no wires or poles, and slowdown instead of blackouts. The PC game's poles were the thing judged too fiddly for a thumb.
  • your call Zoom. Two levels by pinch, or none and a board that always fits by panning.
  • your call No reading needed to play, so a child can; the palette is pictures and the only words are on cards.
  • your call The regrow. Four minutes a tree makes the second plate 25 minutes of play; two minutes makes it twelve.
  • your call Rocks never run out and trees regrow. Nothing on a plate is ever used up, so a plate bought is never a mistake.
