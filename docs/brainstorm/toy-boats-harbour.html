Toy Boats · concept, second pass · 31 Oct 2026 · nothing in Godot yet

Your water is the stage; the chart is a sheet you pull up to aim

Your pond fills the screen, with your five boats floating on it as real boats: a barge, a ferry, a tug, a sailboat and a rowboat, bobbing on the water. One toy cannon sits on a jetty at the foot and fires up, off the top of the screen, at the other player's water. Their shots come back from above: a shadow grows on your water, then the pebble drops. The other fleet is never a board on screen. It is a paper chart that unfolds when it is your turn, where you pinpoint the square and scribble your plans, and folds away the moment you let go.

The mock on the left plays a stretch of a game on its own. Rules, computer and online wire are untouched; the calls at the bottom are what this page needs from you.

One round, as the mock shows it

1
Your turn: the chart unfolds from its corner over the water. Slide to the square, its row and column light, the coordinate reads above the thumb. 0.0–1.5 s
2
Let go. The chart folds back to its corner, the cannon swivels to the column, the fuse sparks, pop, recoil, puff. The pebble climbs, shrinks and leaves by the top of the screen. 1.5–2.7 s
3
A beat later the answer comes back: a toast with the coordinate and a pin or ring on the folded chart. A sinking crosses the boat off the chart's fleet strip with a sparkle. +0.3 s
4
Their shot: a shadow swells on your water where it will land, the pebble drops in from above the top bar. A miss is rings; a hit is a puff, a peg on the deck and the boat rocking hard. Sunk, it tips and settles under the water. +0.6 s
5
Between shots the water is alive: boats bob and swing a little, wakes pulse, light drifts across the surface, as a _process on one mesh.

A round stays about 3 s before thinking, as today. The lay-out happens on the same big water, so the two grids never swap places any more.

The chart tools

A tray of round buttons down the unfolded chart's left edge (the thumb side stays clear). Every tool writes on a planning layer that is the player's own: it is never sent, never judged, and the bin wipes it. The crosshair is the default and the chart folds after each shot.

What this costs and what it keeps

Untouched

  • boats_rules.gd, boats_ai.gd, the seal and the wire, the Firebase rules and tests/_probe_boats.gd.
  • The name, the pebble, the five boat names, the locale rows (one new row for the coordinate tag, three for the tools' captions).
  • Three levels plus Online and a friend; who throws first swapping.

Rebuilt

  • The board: the water and its grid, five boat drawings, the jetty and cannon, the chart folded and unfolded, the tray. Baked meshes under transforms; one live mesh while a pebble, the cannon, a shadow or a stencil moves; strokes baked on lift.
  • The screen to the daily layout: world full rect, FlatTopBar and the tools row, no plates, no deck frame. The lay-out state drags boats on the big water.
  • The tutorial's five pages redrawn through the new board's own motions; the Versus card and the how-to shot.

Budget

  • Today 67–78 draw calls at the board. Estimate 90–120: the water one mesh, boats one baked mesh each under their bob transform, the chart two (paper, marks), strokes one, the letters as today.
  • Bobbing and wakes are a _process on cached meshes so they survive the rest rule; the cannon swivels under a transform, never a rebuild.
  • No instance uniform, ANGLE checked, 810 × 1440 harness.

Sound and feel

  • The shot is the lip pop (a thing taken off) a fuse-length after the finger lifts; the answer coming back is the token (a pin set down) or a pencil scratch with air; sunk keeps its muffled note. Their pebble landing is the token again, deeper. Nothing is water, as today.
  • Haptics: a tap on fire, GOOD on a hit, the heavier one on a sinking.
  • A toy spring cannon flicking pebbles, no fire, no shells: cozy stays the brief unless call 2 says otherwise.

Calls this page is for

  1. The chart unfolds on its own when your turn comes (as in the mock), or stays folded until you tap it? Recommended: on its own, folding on release, so a round is still one gesture.
  2. How naval it gets. A toy spring cannon and pebbles with pops (as drawn), or a real little cannon with a flash, smoke and a boom? Recommended: toy, to keep the house sound and the name honest.
  3. Plan tools only, or shots too. The tray changes no rule. A once-a-game spread or spyglass means new messages on the wire, a smarter computer and a rules deploy. Recommended: tools first, judge on the phone, shots as a second pass if it still feels flat.
  4. Fire on release, or aim then press. Release keeps today's 3 s round. A Fire button on the jetty feels more like a gun deck and allows a re-aim, at one tap more a round. Recommended: release.
  5. Where your answer shows. A toast plus a mark on the folded chart (as drawn), or the chart unfolds again for a second to show the pin land? Recommended: toast and mark; the water stays the stage.
  6. Five kinds of boat (as drawn), or one hull in five lengths with the paint line, as today? Five kinds are five drawings and five tutorial pictures; they also make "a boat is named only when it sinks" read at a glance on your own water.
