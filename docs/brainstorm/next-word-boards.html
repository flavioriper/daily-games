The market read

  • LinkedIn says word games are its weakest. Its games lead told GamesBeat that Pinpoint and Crossclimb, the two word games, are the least popular of its lineup, Zip is the most, and the last four releases were logic. Then it shipped Wend anyway, so it has not given up on words.
  • At NYT the ones that stuck are Connections, Spelling Bee, the Mini and Wordle. Connections overtook Wordle in social chatter. The September 2026 bonus drop (Wordle in 1, Connections 3×3) remixes those four rather than adding genres.
  • The indie hits of the last two years are mechanical, not editorial: Waffle (swap letters), Quartiles on Apple News+ (combine fragments), Typeshift and Knotwords on Puzzmo, Letter Boxed. All of them are made from a dictionary, no clue writer needed.
  • The editorial hits need an author every day: Bracket City at The Atlantic (17 nested clues a day), Apple's Emoji Game (pun phrases), Crossclimb (clued ladder), any crossword. In three languages that is the night pipeline writing and reviewing daily, and puns and nested idioms do not translate.
  • Brazil: Termo had 450k players a day by 2022 and is still covered daily in 2026; Contexto, the guess-by-meaning game, is Brazilian-made. A pt-first audience knows both.
  • Most "best word games 2026" pages are SEO listicles that rank their own game first. The ranking below rests on the few trade pieces (GamesBeat, Engadget, Apple's own guide) and on the house's rules, not on those lists.

Shortlist, in build order

Each runs on a list the house already ships, so the generator is pure list work and pt/es cost nothing new. Round length is the target for Medium. House names are proposals; the original's name is said once, to forbid it.

1

Cross Stitch

recommended firstfollows Waffle

Six five-letter words on a sampler, three across and three down, crossing on a lattice with four empty holes. The letters are stitched in the wrong places. Drag a letter onto another to swap them. A letter in its right hole sits green, one on the right thread but the wrong hole amber, a stray grey. Fifteen swaps; a perfect solve takes ten.

Gesture
drag one tile onto another, one thumb
Round
2 to 4 minutes
Runs on
Hidden Word's answers (en 968, pt 746, es 762) for the six words, its accept lists for nothing; the grid needs only six answers that cross
Generator
easy: search for three across and three down words that agree on nine crossings, then shuffle with a fixed number of swaps so the minimum is known
Stars
swaps left against the minimum; a swap that places two letters is the skill
Levels
Easy a 5×5 with two words given; Medium 5×5; Hard 7×7 (ten words, more swaps); Insane Loose Thread: no amber, only green and grey
Shares
as a colour grid, the way Hidden Word does
The sampler: six words cross on a 5×5 with four holes. Green sits, amber is on the right thread, grey is a stray.
2

Padlock

follows Typeshift

A combination padlock with five letter dials. Each dial holds three to five letters; slide a dial up or down so the row across the middle spells a word. Every letter on every dial belongs to at least one word. Spell them all and the lock opens.

Gesture
vertical drag on one dial, one thumb, nothing to type
Round
1 to 3 minutes
Runs on
Hidden Word's answers for the dealt words, its accept lists (en 15,921, pt 26,792, es 31,208) to credit any other real word the dials line up
Generator
trivial: pick four to six answers, stack their letters by column, shuffle each dial; count the full set of words the dials can make so the day's total is honest
Stars
dialled words against the minimum needed to use every letter; extra real words are bonus clicks, not penalties
Levels
Easy four-letter dials; Medium five; Hard six with deeper dials; Insane Rusty Lock: one dial is stuck and only turns one way
Shares
a column of lit tumblers
Five dials; the window reads PEARL. Slide dials until every letter has been in the window inside a word.
3

Biscuit Tin

follows Quartiles

Twenty broken biscuit pieces in a tin, each a chunk of two to four letters. Tap pieces in order to make a word from one to four of them. Five whole biscuits are hidden in the tin, each made of exactly four pieces; find all five and the tin is empty. Shorter words score along the way, so there is always something to do.

Gesture
tap pieces, tap the plate to serve; nothing to type
Round
3 to 6 minutes
Runs on
Honeycomb's wordfreq dictionaries (en 23,164, pt 33,098, es 34,206 words, rude lists already applied) for scoring; the five long words are chosen from the same lists' common tier
Generator
easy: pick five 8-to-13-letter words, cut each into four chunks, score every ordered combination of up to four pieces against the dictionary; reject a deal whose chunks spell a rude word
Stars
points to the day's line, like Honeycomb's jar; all five biscuits is the solve
Levels
Easy three biscuits and bigger chunks; Medium five; Hard five with two-letter chunks; Insane Crumbs: a sixth biscuit's pieces are mixed in and never fit
Shares
five biscuits, whole or broken
Twenty pieces; WIN + DOW + SI + LL is one whole biscuit. SHA + DOW, TAB + LE and BUT + TER score on the way.
4

Stepping Stones

follows the word ladder (Weaver, Crossclimb)

A brook with a word on each bank. Lay stones across: each stone is a word one letter different from the stone before. Reach the far bank in as few stones as you can. Any real word is a stone, so there is no wrong answer, only long ways round. Later, the night pipeline can add a clue to each stone for a Hard band that plays like the clued version.

Gesture
phone keyboard, as Five Clues and Hidden Word type
Round
1 to 3 minutes
Runs on
Word Trail's four-letter lists (en 160, pt 229, es 217) are too thin; a four-letter cut of the Honeycomb dictionaries gives thousands a language, and the accept lists judge the typed stones
Generator
easy: breadth-first search over the one-letter-apart graph; deal a pair whose shortest path is 4 to 6 and has several routes, so the solve is not a single line
Stars
stones laid against the shortest path, the exact metric Market Sums uses
Levels
Easy three stones with the middle one given; Medium four letters; Hard five letters; Insane Slippery: a stone may change one letter or swap two neighbours, and the path is longer
Shares
a row of stones with the count
COLD to WARM in three stones. A longer way round is still a crossing, just fewer stars.
5

Garden Fence

follows Letter Boxed

Twelve letters on the posts of a square fence, three to a side. String words from post to post; two letters in a row may never come from the same side, and each word starts with the last letter of the one before. Use every post and the fence is strung. The day says how many words that should take.

Gesture
tap the posts in turn, or type; the string draws itself across the garden
Round
3 to 6 minutes; the longest round on this page
Runs on
Honeycomb's dictionaries, no new list
Generator
moderate: deal twelve letters, check a two-word solution exists (the par), reject deals whose best is worse than three words
Stars
words used against par, two words being the day's best
Levels
Easy a triangle of nine posts; Medium the square; Hard a pentagon; Insane Gate: one post is the gate and must be the last letter used
Shares
the strung fence
Twelve posts, three a side. MOIST crosses a side on every step; TUSK carries on from its T.

Authored content, ranked below

These are the formats people talk about most, and each needs words written for it every day in three languages. The night pipeline (Haiku writes, Opus reviews) can do it, as it does for Golden Acorn, Four Shelves and Five Clues, but the credits ran out on 2026-10-29 and the pipeline's move is not settled. Build these after the five above, and only once the night is paid for.

Mini crossword (the NYT Mini)a 5×5 grid from the lists, then ten clues a daylater The most played format after Wordle, and the grid itself is list work. The clues are the cost: ten a day, three languages, each one reviewed. Jam-jar clues from a model read flat without an editor.

Left alone

  • Wend, Squaredle, Blossom, Conexo, Quordle, Pairdle, Phrazle, Crosswordle: a card on the grid already, or a mode of one (see the first table).
  • Redactle: a redacted encyclopedia article, English only, twenty minutes a round.
  • Tiled Words: rotate tiles to rebuild a crossword; needs clues, and the rotation gesture is Pinwheel's.
  • Anagram jumble (newspaper style): cheap, but Honeycomb and Padlock already cover unscrambling with more to do.
  • Any guess-and-feedback code board: removed on 2026-10-13 and not to be re-proposed, whatever the catalogue says about Wordle's skeleton.
