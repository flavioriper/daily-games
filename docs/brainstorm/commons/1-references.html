Cozy Conquest References

What Life and Lemon actually is, what to borrow from it, and the games that already do "play a card, a piece lands on a board, hold the ground" with two or more people taking turns.

1 · The starting point

Life and Lemon is cozy and card-on-field, but not multiplayer, not turns, not conquest

Life and Lemon by Finn Truong (solo, Unity + Illustrator, name-your-price on itch, browser/Windows/Android, "coming to Steam") is a single-player cozy fruit-farming card game. You drag crop cards onto an orchard field, wait for growth, press-and-hold to harvest everything, sell, and fuse cards to breed new species (80+ cards, hidden traits, 15+ characters, biomes). There are no turns, no opponent, no win condition and no way to lose. It ran #1 overall at the GJL Game Parade Spring 2025 jam (108 entries) on presentation, and 4.5/5 from 176 itch ratings.

The gap the judges named is exactly the thing you want to add.

One judge: "lacking depth beyond placement", suggesting terrain bonuses and cross-pollination when crops sit next to each other. A feedback-thread commenter: the game "lacks goals and any possibility of defeat". A turn-based opponent contesting the same field is one answer to both.

Worth borrowing

  • Card → thing on the field. The card is the only control; placement is the whole move. Fits a phone and fits Match's one-move-a-turn wire.
  • Fusion as progression. Two cards make a third; the result is a surprise. In a conquest game that is "merge two sprouts on adjacent plots into a stronger plant".
  • Press-and-hold harvest. The one input players singled out as satisfying. A turn's end-of-turn gather could be the same hold.
  • Register. Flat Illustrator shapes, fruit icons, smooth jazz, soft pops. Reviewers said "moreish" and "played far longer than I meant to"; that is the tone a cozy Versus board should hit.
  • Avoid its faults. Fast growth read as "action for the sake of action"; tiny hitboxes; no tutorial; fusion that resets rarity felt punishing.

3 · The finding

"Turn-based tower-defense conquest with cards" is nearly empty

Two searches for a turn-based, multiplayer, card-driven tower-defense conquest game on mobile found only real-time games: Tower Duel (5-minute matches, 5 tower + 5 attacker cards), War of Towers (real-time card PvP), Conquest Tale (merge cards to spawn units, RTS, unreleased). Card Conquest on Steam is a solo roguelite due 2027. Nothing cozy, nothing turn-based, nothing on a shared board.

The nearest turn-based mechanics for the same feeling are Stormbound's front line (your pieces push a row forward each turn, your spawn area grows with them) and Spirit Island's invader clock (a threat that advances on a fixed schedule unless you hold the land). Both are a turn apart from tower defense, which is what you want.

4 · Fit to this codebase

It drops onto the Versus wire as it stands

Transport

versus/online/match.gd is already a turn's wire: a move, then the other seat's, a minute each, the rules refusing a write out of turn (docs/agents/versus.md). Air Hockey could not go online because it has no turns; a card board has nothing but turns. One message a turn, as Dominoes does.

Friends and strangers

The Friends sheet invites a friend into a live Versus game, and the lobby finds a stranger. A ninth Versus card gets both for free. The live rules are deployed by you (tools/deploy_live.sh), so Online stays off until then.

House rules that bite

Renamed genre: the board never says Faeria, Stormbound or Snap in code or on screen. A house set, not stock pieces. Pieces coloured by seat, so state is a halo or ring, never a shade. World fills the screen. Characters are code (ui/faces/).

Before a spec

A chosen direction gets a tab in docs/brainstorm/concepts.html first, with a playable canvas mock, before any Godot code. The computer opponent comes first (every Versus card has one), the live seat after.

5 · Three directions

Concepts that inherit Life and Lemon and borrow one shape each

A · The Allotment Manahex / Faeria shape

board
A hex allotment, ~5 across, two gardeners at opposite corners. Plots are bare soil at first.
a turn
Play one seed card onto a plot touching ground you hold; it sprouts there. Then every plant grows one step and spreads to an empty neighbour if ripe.
territory
A plot is yours while your plant stands on it. Ripe plants beside a water plot (the well) draw an extra card.
fight
No damage numbers. A bigger plant shades a smaller neighbour, which wilts a step a turn. Thorns block spread. Two of your sprouts on adjacent plots can fuse into a tree.
win
Hold more plots after 12 turns, or hold the other corner.
why
Closest to L&L's field. Fusion is the progression. Nothing dies violently; it wilts.

B · Hedgerow Stormbound shape, the "tower defense" one

board
A 4-wide, 6-deep strip between two cottages. Each player's half fills from their end.
a turn
Play up to your sun (1 on turn 1, +1 a turn, cap 6) in cards onto any row up to your hedge line. Then your critters hop one row toward the other cottage.
territory
Your hedge line is the furthest row you have a critter on. It is also where you may plant.
fight
A hopping critter that meets another nudges it back a row (bigger wins, ties stay). Towers are plant cards that do not hop and nudge everything that passes.
win
Six critters reach the other cottage, or most ground at turn 15.
why
Reads at once as attack/defend. Fits a 1-minute turn. The only direction the market gap actually names.

C · Three Beds Marvel Snap shape, short

board
Three flower beds across the screen, each with a hidden soil rule revealed on turns 1, 2, 3 (sandy: roots count double; shaded: nothing above 3 grows).
a turn
Both players plant face-down at once, then reveal. Energy equals the turn number. Six turns.
territory
A bed belongs to whoever has more bloom in it at the end.
fight
None directly; cards buff your bed, wither theirs, or move a plant across beds.
win
Two beds of three.
why
Three minutes, async-proof (both move each turn), easiest to balance. Least "board", least "conquest".
Recommendation: B, Hedgerow, with A's fusion as its one twist.

It is the only shape that matches "tower defense conquest", it has no numbers to read on a phone (a nudge is a nudge), and Stormbound proved the lane-push loop holds up for years on mobile. A is the better pure board game and the better fallback if B plays too flat in the canvas mock.
