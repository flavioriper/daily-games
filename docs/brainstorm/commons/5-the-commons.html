The Commons

One big shared allotment, different every match. Up to four gardeners start in the corners under cloud, farm sun and blossom from the plots they hold, hatch critters to push out and shoo each other back, and each keeps a sealed goal nobody else can see. The feel is the farming card game you liked: a card is the only control, the plot you put it on is the whole decision, nothing dies, things wilt and run off. The strategy layer under it is the one from the base-building war games, with a board and turns instead of a clock. Drag the map to pan. Tap anything for its card. Tap a critter for where it can go.

A different board every match

The map is dealt from a seed: three streams wander across it, meadows and orchards come in clusters, dens and seed caches are scattered, a few lakes break the ground. The Old Oak stands in the middle; whoever has a plant beside it draws an extra card a turn, and two goals in the pack want it. Every third turn a season card turns face up and changes the rules for three turns. Neutral wilds wander out of the dens and bother everyone the same.

Sealed goals

Each gardener is dealt two sealed goals at the start and keeps one. Nobody sees it until it is done. Complete it while still holding your cottage and the match ends with you ahead; if nobody finishes by turn 20, the most plots held wins. Six of the pack:

Farm to hatch, hatch to push

Two things to farm. ☀ Sun comes every turn, 2 plus one per meadow you hold, and pays for cards. ✿ Blossom is a stock: every orchard plot you hold adds one a turn, and critters cost blossom to hatch at a burrow or hive you hold. A critter is a piece that moves. Fights have no numbers: the bigger critter shoos the smaller one back a plot, equals both wilt a step, a critter at nothing goes home. Plants still hold ground; critters hold ground only while they stand on it.

The deck

Thirty cards a gardener, the same deck for everyone, in four kinds. Seeds and buildings go on free uncovered ground touching ground you hold. Critter cards hatch at one of your burrows or hives. Tools go on your own pieces.

A turn with four people in it

Everyone plays at once. Each gardener sends one message a turn: the cards played and where, and where each critter walks. The server resolves all four in one fixed order, then everyone sees the new board. A turn can take a minute live or a day by post; nobody waits on anybody.

1 · OrdersPlay cards onto plots, point critters at plots within their reach. Sent as one message. Until everyone has sent, nothing moves.
2 · The garden growsSun and blossom are paid out. Plants grow, shaded ones wilt, trees drop sprouts, clouds lift where ground is held.
3 · Critters walkAll moves happen together. Two critters on one plot: bigger shoos smaller back the way it came. A critter on a plant of another gardener's wilts it a step.
4 · Wilds and the seasonDens let out their wilds, wilds walk toward the nearest plant, scarecrows send them off. Every third turn the next season card turns up.

What this needs from the house

A new room on the server. Today's live match is two seats taking turns, one move then the other's, with the rules refusing a write out of turn. This is four seats writing one order each, and a resolve step that runs once all four are in (or a clock runs out). New rules, deployed by you. Friends invites and the lobby carry over as they are.

The camera exists. Tally's sheet already pans and zooms a field far bigger than the screen and draws only the squares in view, in batches. A 400-plot map with clouds, plants and critters lives inside the 855 draw calls the same way.

A computer gardener for the other three seats. Every Versus card has one; this one needs a cheap order-writer that pushes toward the nearest resource and fences its den. Solo play and a two-player match on a four-corner map both work with bots in the empty corners.

Untuned, all of it. Map size, how far clouds lift, critter reach, blossom rates, twenty turns. The next step is this page as a tab in the brainstorm book and a canvas mock played a few dozen times.
