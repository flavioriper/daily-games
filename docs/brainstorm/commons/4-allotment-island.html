Allotment Island

The field is a floating island, not a grid: plots are square, the shape is ragged, and most of it starts under cloud. You see the plots beside your cottage. Every plot you hold lifts the cloud off its neighbours at the start of your turn, so pushing outward is how you find the stream, the meadows, and the dens. Tap any plot or card to read it. Next turn lifts the thinning clouds.

How the cloud lifts

The starting deck

Twenty-one cards, the same for both gardeners. A card is a seed packet: the picture window shows what it grows. Seeds and the hedge go on free, uncovered ground touching ground you hold. The two tools go on your own plants.

What the cloud changes

The pond is no longer the guaranteed opening. In the storyboard, apple beside water on turn 1 was a tree by turn 2 and seeded the middle for free, every game. Now you cannot see the stream from the cottage. You find it by holding ground toward it, or you don't.

Turns 9 to 12 have a job. The middle of the island is uncovered last, by whoever pushed hardest, and it holds the seed caches and the second den. The late game is the contest for what was just revealed, not two full gardens staring at each other.

Dens are a choice. A hidden den sleeps. The gardener who lifts the cloud off it wakes it, and the first wild heads for the nearest plant, which is usually theirs. Fencing before you push is the tower-defense half, and now it has a reason.

Caches reward the explorer. Lift the cloud off a seed cache and draw a card on the spot. Cards are the limiting resource after sun, so the gardener who uncovers more plays more.

Still untuned. Reveal reach (neighbours only, or two deep?), how many caches, and whether a wild can wander under cloud are all placeholders until the canvas mock is played.
