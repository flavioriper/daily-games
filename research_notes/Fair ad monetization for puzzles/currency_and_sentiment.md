# Premium currency, pay-to-win perception and player sentiment in cozy/daily puzzle games

Scope note: research done 2026-09-29 with ~15 search/fetch calls. Several sources are older than the preferred 2022-2026 window (Tapjoy 2017, Unity ~2016-2019); they are flagged where used. Some aggregator results (Statista paywalled, SEO pages about Wordscapes) were judged low quality and are either excluded or flagged.

## How do daily puzzle products monetize, and what is said about their fairness?

### Takeaway
The prestige daily-puzzle products (NYT Games, Puzzmo, Zach Gage's games, Knotwords) monetize by subscription or one-time/annual unlock with no ads or few ads and a free daily core; mass-market puzzle apps (Sudoku.com, Wordscapes) run ads plus coins/hints, and their reviews complain mainly about ad frequency and timing. None of the "respected" daily brands found sells a premium currency.

### Cited Findings
- NYT Games: over 10 million daily players and over one million premium games subscribers (as of 2024); puzzles played 11.2 billion times in 2025. — [Wikipedia: The New York Times Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times_Games)
- NYT committed on acquiring Wordle (2022) that it "will continue to be free and there will be no changes to its gameplay." — [Kathmandu Post / NYT acquisition coverage](https://kathmandupost.com/world/2022/02/01/new-york-times-acquires-wordle)
- NYT total subscribers 12.33 million (end of Sept 2025, +9%); about half take the ~$30/month all-access bundle; Wordle alone 4.2 billion plays in 2025; YipitData (2023) suggested subscribers spent more time in Games than in the news product; digital-only subscription revenue +14% YoY driven partly by Games. — [Boston Globe, Jan 2026](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/01/21/business/new-york-times-games/)
- Puzzmo: free games, Puzzmo Plus at $3.99/month or $39.99/year unlocks extra games/features, leaderboards, archives and ad-free play; distributed through newspaper partners (Hearst acquired it 2023; Postmedia in Canada at $35/yr vs a $55/yr regular Canadian price). — [ADN](https://www.adn.com/puzzles/2024/06/08/play-traditional-newspaper-games-with-a-new-spin/); [BusinessWire / Postmedia](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240404561282/en/Postmedia-Launches-Puzzmo-Hearst-Newspapers%E2%80%99-Online-Puzzle-Games-Experience-Exclusively-in-Canada)
- Good Sudoku (Zach Gage & Jack Schlesinger): free download, one-time "Full Game Unlock" IAP (reported $3.99-$4.99 depending on source/date); praised for AI hints and removing busywork. — [iMore](https://www.imore.com/zach-gage-releases-good-sudoku-reimagined-and-fun-take-classic); [TouchArcade](https://toucharcade.com/games/good-sudoku-by-zach-gage); [Daring Fireball](https://daringfireball.net/2020/07/good_sudoku_by_zach_gage)
- Knotwords: free, no ads; Twist puzzles, 30 extra monthly puzzlebook puzzles, stats, archive and customization behind one IAP at $4.99/year or $11.99 lifetime. — [Six Colors](https://sixcolors.com/post/2022/04/knotwords-offers-crossword-puzzles-without-clues/)
- Sudoku.com (Easybrain): App Store reviews complain ads appear when finishing or starting a game and break concentration; the developer replies that ads keep it free and points to a paid "without ads" purchase. — [Sudoku.com App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/sudoku-com-number-games/id1193508329?xs=1)
- Wordscapes (PeopleFun): coins buy hints/boosts; coin packs from $0.99 (240 coins) up to $24.99 bundles. — [Common Sense Media / listing aggregate](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/wordscapes) (price detail came from a lower-quality aggregator in the same search; treat as indicative)

### Inferences
- The "premium daily puzzle" positioning (which this game's cozy tone and daily grid resemble) is associated with subscription/unlock and a free, pure daily core. Selling currency that affects the daily boards would put the game in the Wordscapes/Sudoku.com camp rather than the NYT/Puzzmo/Gage camp.
- Across all three price-point models found (Puzzmo sub, Knotwords annual/lifetime, Good Sudoku one-time), what is sold is *more content, archives, stats, and no ads* - not advantage. The current plan (banner + lifetime remove-ads, earned-only gold) already aligns with this camp.

### Gaps
- No published sales/conversion figures found for Good Sudoku, Knotwords, Really Bad Chess or Blossom; no Zach Gage monetization postmortem located in this pass.
- LinkedIn games: confirmed elsewhere as free inside LinkedIn, but not re-sourced here.
- Puzzmo subscriber counts are not public in the sources found.

## Player sentiment on ads: rewarded vs forced, paying to remove ads, uninstalls

### Takeaway
Players consistently prefer opt-in rewarded ads over forced formats, a sizeable minority (roughly 3 in 10 spenders) pay specifically to remove ads, and disruptive ads are a leading uninstall/negative-review driver. Among reasons to quit a game, "pay to win" ranks first in one 2023 North American survey.

### Cited Findings
- Mistplay 2023 (North America): players spend money primarily for progress (61.96%), rare content (30.71%) and ad-free play (29.27%); top reasons for quitting a game are "pay to win" mechanics (59.75%), bugs (55.18%) and lack of progression (54.55%); 28.64% want flexible in-game currency in offers, 45.95% prefer one-time deals. — [GameDev Reports summary of Mistplay](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/mistplay-north-american-players-behaviour)
- AppHarbr player sentiment survey: 84% of players say they uninstall after disruptive ads; 37% have left a negative review over ad quality. (Survey by an ad-quality vendor, so interested party.) — [Neo Radar #12 citing AppHarbr](https://neoads.substack.com/p/neo-radar-12)
- Late-2023 North America survey: ~44% of mobile gamers say they continue play when they meet ads, ~16% would consider stopping. — [Statista (paywalled), via search summary](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1445263/attitudes-ads-f2p-games-north-america)
- Unity survey (older, ~2016-2017): 46% of players prefer incentivized video over full-screen pop-ups (29%), banners (20%) and standard interstitial video (5%); 18% always engage with rewarded video, 44% sometimes. — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/unity-the-majority-of-mobile-gamers-are-open-to-engaging-with-incentivized-video-ads/)
- Tapjoy (2017): 54% prefer apps supported by opt-in rewarded ads vs 32% mandatory ads and 14% paid downloads. (Pre-2022; directional only.) — [Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/press-release/20170613-mobile-gamers-prefer-rewarded-ads-by-4-to-1-margin-tapjoy-research-finds/)
- Omnicom Media Group APAC: most gamers say ads too often cause them to stop playing. — [AdNews](https://adnews.com.au/news/gamers-often-give-up-playing-when-served-mobile-ads)

### Inferences
- A banner-only ad load with a lifetime remove-ads purchase is at the mild end of the spectrum; the ad complaint pattern in Sudoku.com reviews is about interstitials at game start/end, which this game does not use. Adding interstitials would move it into the most-complained-about pattern.
- A rewarded "watch an ad" option is the ad format players dislike least, so offering it as an alternative to paying is consistent with sentiment.
- "Pay to win" being the #1 quit reason (Mistplay) is a caution for any plan to sell advantage-granting currency, even in single-player modes.

### Gaps
- No 2024-2026 primary Unity/Liftoff/Digital Turbine "player attitudes" report was fetched directly; the rewarded-preference numbers above are older. Puzzle-genre-specific breakdowns were not found.
- No reliable figure found for the share of *all* players (not spenders) who would pay to remove ads.

## Pay-to-win perception in single-player, score-based modes

### Takeaway
The research literature ties pay-to-win perception mainly to competitive/multiplayer settings and leaderboards; purchases that affect a ranking others see are read as unfair ("pay to cheat"), while progression aids in solo play are judged less harshly. Evidence specific to single-player arcade scores is thin; transparency (marking boosted scores) is supported indirectly by the finding that perceived price fairness predicts loyalty.

### Cited Findings
- "Pay to Win or Pay to Cheat" (study of competitive online game players): purchases that confer competitive advantage are perceived as unfair, and players distinguish these from legitimate progression aids. — [ResearchGate: Pay to Win or Pay to Cheat](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/365005208_Pay_to_Win_or_Pay_to_Cheat_How_Players_of_Competitive_Online_Games_Perceive_Fairness_of_In-Game_Purchases)
- Perceived price fairness was the strongest predictor of game brand loyalty; P2W perception is linked to lower satisfaction and higher churn, especially among competitive players; server-wide leaderboards are described as "auctions of financial commitment". — [Medium / Bootcamp review of P2W research](https://medium.com/design-bootcamp/product-design-and-psychology-the-exploitation-of-the-pay-to-win-model-in-video-game-design-7df534124d2e) (secondary summary; claims attributed to underlying studies not individually verified)
- Representative German sample (46,136 adults): pay-to-win spending correlates with gambling behaviour among heavy payers. — [PMC: Pay-to-Win Gaming and its Interrelation with Gambling](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9411233/)
- Mistplay 2023: "pay to win" is the top reason players quit (59.75%). — [GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/mistplay-north-american-players-behaviour)
- Puzzmo puts leaderboards behind its paid tier but does not sell in-puzzle advantage (the paid tier sells access, not power). — [ADN](https://www.adn.com/puzzles/2024/06/08/play-traditional-newspaper-games-with-a-new-spin/)

### Inferences
- In this game, Arcade bests are personal (local `arcade.cfg`) and boosted bests are already marked with a leaf; with no shared leaderboard, a paid booster is closer to a "progression aid" than to "pay to cheat". The risk rises sharply if Arcade scores ever go to a shared/crowd leaderboard (as Trestle's daily cost already does): boosted runs should then be excluded or separated.
- Keeping daily puzzles pure (no purchasable hints that affect streak/stats/crowd comparisons) matches every respected daily brand found. Paid hints on the daily boards would touch the streak and the crowd percentile, which is where perceived unfairness concentrates.
- Even solo, the label "pay to win" is emotionally loaded (top quit reason); marketing copy and UI should frame boosters as fun modifiers, and they should never multiply score (already the rule).

### Gaps
- No study found that measures pay-to-win perception specifically in single-player score-attack modes without leaderboards.
- No evidence found on whether marking boosted scores (asterisk/leaf) changes perception; it is a reasonable inference, not a measured result.

## Dual currency (soft earned + hard purchased) vs single currency for a small indie game; hint-pack sales

### Takeaway
No rigorous public data was found on dual- vs single-currency outcomes for small indie puzzle games. What was found: word games like Wordscapes run a single coin currency that is both earned and bought and spent on hints; players in surveys show only moderate appetite for currency (28.6%) vs one-time deals (46%) and large bundles (59.6%). Dark-pattern research flags obscured real-money cost (premium currency) as a monetary dark pattern, and dark-rated games are far more likely to be F2P with IAPs.

### Cited Findings
- Wordscapes: one coin currency, earned and purchasable, spent on hints and boosts; coin packs from $0.99. — [Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/wordscapes) (plus lower-quality aggregators; exact prices indicative)
- Mistplay 2023 offer preferences: 59.55% favour larger bundles, 45.95% one-time deals, 28.64% flexible in-game currency. — [GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/mistplay-north-american-players-behaviour)
- Analysis of 1,496 mobile games rated on darkpatterns.games (85,388 rated pattern instances): monetary dark patterns were the most prominent category in "dark" games (26,330 instances vs 2,781 in "healthy" games); 96.8% of dark games were F2P vs 53% of healthy ones; 93.6% of dark games had IAPs vs 54%; 52.4% had ads vs 37.3%; only 10.76% of all games had zero rated dark patterns. — [arXiv 2412.05039, "Level Up or Game Over"](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.05039v1)
- Zagal, Björk & Lewis's taxonomy groups game dark patterns as temporal, monetary and social; "pay to skip" (paying to bypass challenge or waiting) is a named monetary dark pattern. — [DiGRA / Zagal et al. summary via arXiv 2412.05039](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.05039); [DiGRA 2020 paper](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/156460/1/DiGRA_2020_paper_189.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a small game with ~few purchasable items, dual currency adds a UI layer, an exchange rate to balance, and the "obscured cost" dark-pattern flag, while the evidence suggests players prefer one-time deals. A single earned gold plus direct real-money products (remove ads, a one-time "supporter"/unlock) is simpler and reads fairer.
- Converting the current earned-only gold into a buyable currency would also change its regulatory character in Brazil: the codebase notes Lei 15.211/2025 bans *paid* random items for minors; the current design avoids randomness, which must be kept if gold becomes purchasable.
- Paid hints on dailies are effectively "pay to skip" in Zagal's taxonomy.

### Gaps
- No public data found on hint-pack revenue share in word/puzzle games, on whale dependence for small indie puzzle titles, or on currency inflation in such games.

## "Offer only on loss" / second-chance offers: effectiveness and manipulation perception

### Takeaway
Continue/extra-moves offers on failure are a core monetization lever in match-3 (e.g. Royal Match sells extra moves for in-game or real currency), but dark-pattern literature ties them to loss aversion and "endowed value" manipulation. No quantitative effectiveness data was found in this pass.

### Cited Findings
- Royal Match monetizes extra moves, retries and continues to give another chance at winning, buyable with in-game or real currency. — [esports.net: How does Royal Match make money](https://www.esports.net/news/mobile-games/how-does-royal-match-make-money/); background: [Naavik Royal Match deep dive](https://naavik.co/deep-dives/royal-match/)
- Dark-pattern research describes loss aversion exploited through the "endowed value" pattern: players fear losing progress already gained more than they value new gains. — [arXiv 2412.05039](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.05039)
- "Pay to skip" is classified as a monetary dark pattern (Zagal et al.). — [DiGRA 2020 paper](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/156460/1/DiGRA_2020_paper_189.pdf)

### Inferences
- A second chance paid with *earned* gold, or a rewarded ad offered only after a loss, is a mild version of the pattern; it becomes the manipulative version when (a) it is priced in real money at the moment of loss, (b) the run's near-miss is emphasized with a countdown, or (c) the offer escalates. Keeping it to one offer per run, no timer pressure, and never on daily puzzles (streaks) keeps it on the fair side.
- A rewarded ad as a loss-only fallback fits the rewarded-ad preference data, but offering real-money currency at the same moment would stack loss aversion with payment - the configuration most criticised in the literature.

### Gaps
- No public conversion rates for continue offers, no player-survey data on whether loss-time offers feel manipulative, and no specific "near-miss" monetization study for puzzle games was retrieved.

## Subscription or "supporter" options for indie puzzle games

### Takeaway
Subscription works at scale for bundles with a stream of new content (NYT: 1M+ games subscribers; Puzzmo Plus ~$40/yr), and indie puzzle designers favour a cheap annual plus a lifetime unlock (Knotwords $4.99/yr or $11.99 forever) or a one-time unlock (Good Sudoku). No conversion data for indie supporter tiers was found.

### Cited Findings
- NYT Games: 1M+ premium games subscribers against 10M+ daily players (i.e. roughly a 10% ratio of subscribers to daily players, derived from the two figures). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times_Games)
- Puzzmo Plus $3.99/month or $39.99/year: ad-free, archives, leaderboards, extra games. — [ADN](https://www.adn.com/puzzles/2024/06/08/play-traditional-newspaper-games-with-a-new-spin/)
- Knotwords: $4.99/year or $11.99 lifetime for extras, stats and archives; no ads. — [Six Colors](https://sixcolors.com/post/2022/04/knotwords-offers-crossword-puzzles-without-clues/)
- Good Sudoku: single full-game unlock. — [iMore](https://www.imore.com/zach-gage-releases-good-sudoku-reimagined-and-fun-take-classic)

### Inferences
- For this game, a natural "supporter" product is an upgrade of the existing remove-ads purchase (e.g. lifetime or annual) that also unlocks archives/past days, extra stats, cosmetic skins (the chess skin contract already exists), or "New" deals on boards - content and convenience, never advantage. That mirrors Knotwords/Puzzmo.
- The NYT subscriber-to-player ratio is not transferable to an indie app without the NYT brand; treat it as an upper bound.

### Gaps
- No indie conversion rates for annual vs lifetime supporter tiers; no data on whether a small puzzle app's players accept a subscription at all versus a lifetime unlock.
